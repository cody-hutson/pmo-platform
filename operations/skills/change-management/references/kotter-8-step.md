# Kotter 8-Step Process Reference

## Purpose

This reference is the authoritative source for Kotter's 8-Step organizational-change
process, consumed by the change-management skill as a **selectable methodology** (routed
through `methodology-selection.md`, the cross-methodology selector) and by
`artifact-generator`. It owns the platform treatment of the 8 steps, their
sequence-gating, the per-step assessment readout, the org-wide-process-change selection
boundary (Kotter vs ADKAR / Lewin / Bridges / 7-S), and the unified application
procedure an agent runs to apply Kotter to a real transformation.

Kotter's lens is **organizational and top-down**: it answers "how does leadership lead
the whole organization through a large change," sequentially, from establishing urgency
to anchoring the change in culture. This is distinct from — and complementary to —
ADKAR's *individual-adoption* lens (`adkar-framework.md`, which owns per-person/per-group
readiness scoring). Run Kotter to lead the organization through the transformation; run
ADKAR to confirm each stakeholder group is individually ready. The two meet at Kotter
Steps 5-6 (empower + short-term wins). The cross-methodology selection boundary lives in
§11 and feeds the selector.

## 1. The 8-Step Process (orientation)

Kotter's model is a **sequence**: change is led one stage at a time, and each stage
establishes the conditions the next stage depends on. The eight steps group into three
phases — Steps 1-4 establish the climate for change, Steps 5-6 engage and enable the
organization, Steps 7-8 implement and sustain the change. Kotter's "eight errors" thesis
is that skipping or under-investing in any step — especially an early one — undermines
every step that follows.

| Step | What it is (1 line) | The shift it drives | Primary failure signal if skipped or weak |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **1. Create urgency** | Raise the felt need for change above complacency | Complacency → "we must act now" | Change stalls for lack of energy; "why bother" inertia |
| **2. Build a guiding coalition** | Assemble a powerful team to lead the change | Lone champion → credible coalition | Initiative has no authority; opposition outweighs it |
| **3. Form a strategic vision** | Craft a clear, communicable picture of the future + initiatives | Confusion → a shared direction | Direction is a plan/budget, not a picture; people can't follow it |
| **4. Communicate the vision** | Saturate every channel; lead by example | Vision in a drawer → vision everyone knows | Vision under-communicated; effort fragments |
| **5. Empower broad-based action** | Remove barriers — structures, systems, skill gaps | Blocked staff → enabled "volunteer army" | People want to act but structures/systems block them |
| **6. Generate short-term wins** | Plan for and create visible, unambiguous early wins | Doubt → proof and momentum | Urgency decays; cynics regain the upper hand |
| **7. Consolidate gains / sustain acceleration** | Use win-credibility to drive bigger changes; don't stop | First win → deeper transformation | Premature "mission accomplished"; momentum lost |
| **8. Anchor new approaches in culture** | Root the change in group norms, values, behaviors | New behavior → "the way we work here" | Change reverts when the sponsor leaves; old ways creep back |

**Sequence principle:** Steps 1-4 are the *climate* (urgency, coalition, vision,
communication); Steps 5-6 are the *engagement and enablement* (empower, wins); Steps 7-8
are the *implementation and sustainment* (consolidate, anchor). An under-built early step
caps everything downstream — a coalition assembled before urgency is felt has no mandate,
and wins engineered before the vision is communicated read as random successes rather
than proof of the change.

## 2. Step 1 — Create Urgency

**What it is:** Raise the organization's felt need for change above its baseline
complacency. Kotter's threshold: roughly **75% of leadership** must be genuinely
convinced that the status quo is more dangerous than the change before the effort has
enough energy to start. Urgency is *felt need*, not *manufactured panic*.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Real evidence of threat/opportunity | Leaders and staff can name a concrete, current reason to change | Abstract or future-only rationale; "someday" framing | Surface real, current evidence (market, customer, competitor, cost); bring the outside in |
| Felt need above complacency | The status quo feels riskier than acting | Comfort with current state; "we've always done it this way" | Make the cost of inaction visible and personal |
| True vs false urgency | Purposeful, directed energy | Anxious busy-ness with no direction; meeting churn | Channel energy toward the vision (§3-4), not activity for its own sake |

**Failure mode:** *False urgency* — anxious activity without direction — is as damaging
as complacency. It burns the organization out before the real change begins.

## 3. Step 2 — Build a Guiding Coalition

**What it is:** Assemble a team with enough **position power, expertise, credibility, and
leadership** to drive the change — and ensure it operates *as a team*, not as a committee
that meets and disperses. A guiding coalition needs both management skill (to keep the
effort under control) and leadership skill (to drive change).

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Sufficient power | The coalition can authorize and unblock change | Coalition too junior; line-managers only; easily overruled | Recruit members with the position power the change requires |
| Right mix of skills | Management + leadership + domain expertise + credibility present | All managers, no leaders (or vice versa); missing key expertise | Map the needed skills; recruit across silos to fill gaps |
| Works as a team | Members trust each other and act jointly | Turf protection; members represent only their function | Build trust deliberately — shared activity, off-sites, a common goal |

**Failure mode:** A *coalition in name only* — powerless, junior, or fractured — lets the
change be overruled the first time it meets resistance.

## 4. Step 3 — Form a Strategic Vision

**What it is:** Craft a vision of the future that is **clear, imaginable, feasible,
focused, flexible, and communicable** — paired with the strategic initiatives that will
achieve it. The test: a leader can describe the vision clearly in under five minutes (or
in a single sentence) and get understanding plus interest in return.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| A picture, not a plan | People can visualize the future state | Vision is a budget, a timeline, or a metric — not a picture | Draft the future state as something people can *see* themselves in |
| Communicable in <5 min | The vision survives a one-sentence / five-minute test | Too complex to repeat; needs slides to explain | Sharpen until it is repeatable from memory |
| Paired with initiatives | The vision has concrete strategic initiatives behind it | Aspiration with no path to achieve it | Attach the initiatives that move the org toward the vision |

**Failure mode:** A vision that is actually a *plan or a number* gives no one a reason to
follow it; people comply with plans but commit to pictures.

## 5. Step 4 — Communicate the Vision

**What it is:** Communicate the vision through **every available channel, relentlessly**,
and — most importantly — **lead by example**. Kotter's observation: visions are typically
under-communicated by a factor of **10 to 100×**. The goal is to enlist a "volunteer
army" — a broad base of people who understand the vision and choose to act on it.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Saturation across channels | The vision is repeated everywhere people look | One-and-done memo; single town hall, then silence | Repeat relentlessly across every channel; weave it into routine communication |
| Walk the talk | Leaders' behavior matches the vision | Leaders act inconsistently with the vision — the most corrosive failure | Make leader behavior visibly consistent; address inconsistencies openly |
| Enlist the volunteer army | A broad base understands and acts | Only the coalition carries the message | Equip many voices to carry the vision, not just the top |

**Failure mode:** *Behavioral inconsistency* by leaders is more destructive than weak
messaging — one visible contradiction undoes a hundred consistent communications.

**Composes with:** `comms-writer` owns the *channel mechanics and message drafting* for
change communications; this step owns the *Kotter saturation rule* (communicate 10-100×
more than feels necessary) and the *lead-by-example obligation*. Reference `comms-writer`
for the message build; do not restate channel-design mechanics here.

## 6. Step 5 — Empower Broad-Based Action

**What it is:** Remove the barriers that stop the volunteer army from acting on the
vision — **hostile structures and systems, disempowering supervisors, and skill gaps**.
Empowerment is structural, not motivational: people already want to act (Steps 1-4 built
that); this step clears their path.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Aligned structures/systems | Org structures, processes, and systems support the vision | Structures still reward the old way; systems block the new way | Realign structures, incentives, and systems to the vision |
| Blockers confronted | Disempowering managers are coached or moved | A few managers tolerated in undermining the change | Confront blockers visibly; do not let exceptions signal that resistance is safe |
| Skill gaps closed | People have the training and tools to act | Capability gap left unaddressed | Supply the skills, tools, and authority the new way requires |

**Failure mode:** Leaving structural blockers in place tells the volunteer army the
change is not real — they revert to the path the systems still reward.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *individual* Ability and Knowledge
elements and the change-champion network sizing — the per-person capability layer.
Kotter Step 5 (org-level barrier removal) and ADKAR Ability/Knowledge (per-person
capability) compose: clear the structural barriers here, confirm each group can actually
perform via ADKAR. Reference `adkar-framework.md` for the Ability/Knowledge scoring and
champion sizing; do not restate them here.

## 7. Step 6 — Generate Short-Term Wins

**What it is:** **Plan for and deliberately create** visible, unambiguous wins clearly
related to the change, typically within **6-18 months**. Wins are not luck — they are
engineered. They provide proof the change works, reward the effort, build momentum, and
disarm cynics and resisters.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Wins are planned | Early wins are deliberately engineered, not hoped for | "We'll see results eventually"; no planned early wins | Plan specific, achievable early wins into the roadmap |
| Wins are visible & unambiguous | The win is clearly attributable to the change | Claimed wins are vague, contested, or invisible | Choose wins that are unmistakable and clearly tied to the change |
| Win-deliverers recognized | The people who produced the wins are visibly rewarded | Wins happen but no one is recognized | Recognize and reward the contributors publicly |

**Failure mode:** With no early wins, *urgency decays and the coalition loses
credibility* — the change becomes "the thing leadership talked about that went nowhere."

## 8. Step 7 — Consolidate Gains / Sustain Acceleration

**What it is:** Use the **credibility from early wins to tackle bigger changes**, and
critically — **do not declare victory too soon**. The first wins are a platform for
deeper change, not the finish line. Premature victory is one of Kotter's most-cited
failure modes: it stops the effort before the change is irreversible, and the
organization regresses.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Press harder after wins | Momentum is reinvested in bigger changes | "Mission accomplished" at the first win | Use win-credibility to take on the harder, structural changes |
| Don't declare victory early | The effort continues until the change is durable | Celebration treated as completion; resources withdrawn | Keep urgency up; reinvigorate with new projects and agents of change |
| Eliminate residual resistance | Remaining structural barriers are cleared | Old systems/policies left half-changed | Use the credibility to finish realigning structures (carries §5 forward) |

**Failure mode:** *Declaring victory too early* converts a near-win into a regression —
the change reverses precisely because the effort relaxed at the moment it needed to push.

**Composes with:** `hypercare-plan.md` (Anti-Patterns, the "Declaring victory at go-live"
row) already owns the premature-victory statistic in the go-live context (a majority of
major initiatives fail partly due to premature victory; track adoption past go-live).
That row is the operational application of this step at deployment time — reference it;
do not restate the statistic or the row here.

## 9. Step 8 — Anchor New Approaches in Culture

**What it is:** Make the change stick by rooting it in the organization's **group norms,
values, and shared behaviors**. Culture changes **last** — only after the new behaviors
have produced visible results does the culture absorb them as "the way we work here."
Anchoring requires tying the change to onboarding, promotion, and leadership succession
so it survives turnover.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Behavior → results → culture | New behaviors persist because they demonstrably work | Trying to mandate culture change first, before results | Make the connection between the new behaviors and the results explicit and repeated |
| Embedded in people systems | Hiring, onboarding, and promotion reflect the new way | New behaviors absent from hiring/promotion criteria | Embed the new norms in onboarding, promotion, and recognition |
| Survives leadership change | The change outlasts its original sponsor | Change reverts when the sponsor departs | Ensure leadership succession reinforces, not reverses, the change |

**Failure mode:** A change *not anchored in culture* reverts the moment its sponsor leaves
or attention shifts — the new way was never made into the default.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *individual* Reinforcement element
(sustaining the new behavior per person, measured past go-live). Kotter Step 8
(organizational culture anchoring) and ADKAR Reinforcement (individual reinforcement)
compose: anchor the change in the culture at the org level here, sustain it per person
via ADKAR Reinforcement. Reference `adkar-framework.md` for the Reinforcement scoring and
measurement; do not restate them here.

## 10. Sequencing & Stage-Gate Rules

The Kotter analogue of ADKAR's barrier-point gating. The 8 steps are a **sequence-gate**:
a later step is undermined when an earlier step is incomplete. Assess each step on a
five-level readout — mirroring ADKAR's 1-5 scale to keep the methodology suite
consistent.

### Per-Step Status Readout (1-5)

| Status | Label | What it means for the step |
|--------|-------|----------------------------|
| 5 | **Strong** | Step fully achieved; the condition it establishes is solidly in place |
| 4 | **Adequate** | Step met; minor gaps that do not threaten downstream steps |
| 3 | **Borderline** | Step partially done; at risk under pressure; needs reinforcement |
| 2 | **Weak** | Significant gaps; the condition this step establishes is not reliably present |
| 1 | **Absent** | Step not done; downstream steps have no foundation to build on |

### Sequence-Gate Logic

```
FOR the change effort:
  Assess Step 1 (Urgency)             status 1-5
  Assess Step 2 (Coalition)           status 1-5
  Assess Step 3 (Vision)              status 1-5
  Assess Step 4 (Communicate)         status 1-5
  Assess Step 5 (Empower)             status 1-5
  Assess Step 6 (Short-term wins)     status 1-5
  Assess Step 7 (Consolidate)         status 1-5
  Assess Step 8 (Anchor)              status 1-5

  earliest_weak_step = first step, in 1 -> 8 order, with status <= 3

  Phase diagnosis:
    IF any of Steps 1-4 <= 3:  CLIMATE NOT ESTABLISHED
       -> do not push Steps 5-8 hard; fix the earliest weak climate step first
    ELSE IF Step 5 or 6 <= 3:  NO MOMENTUM
       -> wins will not materialize; clear barriers (5) / engineer wins (6) before consolidating
    ELSE IF Step 7 or 8 <= 3:  CHANGE WILL REGRESS
       -> sustain acceleration (7) / anchor in culture (8) before treating the change as done

  ACTION = the per-step intervention for earliest_weak_step (see table below)
```

**Address-the-earliest-weak-step-first rule:** Like ADKAR's barrier point, the constraint
is the *earliest* weak step in sequence — investing in a later step while an earlier one
is weak wastes the investment (engineering wins while the vision is uncommunicated
produces wins no one can interpret).

### The Three Cardinal Kotter Errors

| Error | What it looks like | Why it breaks the change |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| **Skipping steps** | Jumping to action (Step 5+) without urgency, coalition, or vision | The change has no energy, no mandate, and no direction |
| **Under-communicating** | Communicating the vision 10-100× less than required | The volunteer army never forms; the effort fragments |
| **Declaring victory early** | Treating the first wins (Step 6) as completion | Momentum collapses; the change regresses before it is durable |

### Per-Step Intervention (when each step is the earliest weak step)

| Earliest weak step | Intervention before advancing |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| **1. Urgency** | Surface real current evidence of threat/opportunity; raise felt need above complacency |
| **2. Coalition** | Recruit members with sufficient power and the right skill mix; build them into a real team |
| **3. Vision** | Sharpen the vision into a communicable picture (<5 min test); pair it with initiatives |
| **4. Communicate** | Saturate every channel; make leader behavior visibly consistent with the vision |
| **5. Empower** | Realign structures/systems; confront blockers; close skill and tool gaps |
| **6. Short-term wins** | Engineer visible, unambiguous early wins; recognize the people who deliver them |
| **7. Consolidate** | Press harder after wins; do not relax; reinvest credibility in bigger changes |
| **8. Anchor** | Embed new norms in onboarding/promotion; tie the change to leadership succession |

## 11. When to Use Kotter vs ADKAR / Lewin / Bridges / 7-S

This is the **cross-methodology selection surface** — the primary feed to the
methodology selector (`methodology-selection.md`, the cluster-canonical home for choosing
among the change methodologies; it reads the differentiators below to recommend a
methodology for a given change). Until that selector is consulted, this table is the
Kotter-anchored view of the boundary. The two selection axes are **unit of change** (what
the methodology operates on) and **dominant risk** (the risk it is best suited to manage).

| Methodology | Unit of change | Temporal shape | Dominant risk it manages | Best when | Complements |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|--------------------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Kotter (8-Step)** | The organization (top-down) | Sequence (8 steps) | Large transformation stalls / loses momentum | Org-wide, urgency-driven process change; you need to *lead the org through* a big transformation | Lewin (the frame it operationalizes); ADKAR (individual readiness underneath) |
| **ADKAR** | The individual (per person/group) | Sequence (5 elements) | A group is not individually ready to adopt | You need to know *whether each group is ready* and where each is blocked | Kotter (org-level transformation it sits inside); Bridges (the emotional layer) |
| **Lewin (3-Stage)** | The change state | State model (unfreeze → change → refreeze) | Over-complicating a simple change | You need the simplest change-state frame; Kotter 1-4 ≈ unfreeze, 5-7 ≈ change, 8 ≈ refreeze | Kotter (operationalizes it); everything (it is the meta-frame) |
| **Bridges (Transition)** | The person's psychology | Phase model (Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) | Emotional/identity disruption is mishandled | Identity or emotional disruption is the dominant risk under any structural change | Runs underneath Kotter and ADKAR (the human-experience layer) |
| **7-S** | The org's seven elements | Static snapshot (no sequence) | Org elements drift out of alignment | You need to *diagnose alignment* across Strategy/Structure/Systems/Shared-values/Style/Staff/Skills before or after a change | Any sequence model (checks alignment at any point) |

**Kotter ↔ ADKAR complementarity (they are complementary, not competing):** Kotter is the
*org-process* lens (lead the whole organization through the transformation, top-down,
in sequence); ADKAR is the *individual-change* lens (confirm each stakeholder group is
individually ready, person by person). They are run together, not chosen between: Kotter
leads the organization through the change while ADKAR confirms each group can adopt it.
The two **meet at Kotter Steps 5-6** — empowering broad-based action (Step 5) and
generating short-term wins (Step 6) are exactly where org-level enablement and
individual-level Ability/Desire intersect. Lewin is the frame both sit inside; Bridges
runs underneath all of them (the people-experience layer); 7-S checks alignment at any
point in the sequence.

**Selection summary:** choose **Kotter** when the unit of change is the *whole
organization* and the dominant risk is a *large transformation stalling*; choose **ADKAR**
when the unit is the *individual/group* and the dominant risk is *non-adoption*; choose
**Lewin** for the simplest state frame; **Bridges** when *emotional transition* is the
risk; **7-S** when the need is an *alignment diagnostic* rather than a change sequence.

## 12. Unified Application Procedure

This is the deterministic procedure an agent executes to apply Kotter to a real change.
It ties §§1-10 into a runnable flow.

```
1. CONFIRM Kotter is the right methodology per §11:
   - unit of change = the whole organization (top-down)
   - the change is a large, urgency-driven transformation
   - dominant risk = the transformation stalling or losing momentum
   (If the unit is the individual/group, route to ADKAR; if the need is an
    alignment snapshot, route to 7-S; see §11.)

2. ASSESS the current status of each of the 8 steps (Strong -> Absent, 1-5 per §10).

3. COMPUTE the earliest weak step per §10:
   earliest_weak_step = first step (1 -> 8) with status <= 3, else "None".
   Derive the phase diagnosis (CLIMATE NOT ESTABLISHED / NO MOMENTUM /
   CHANGE WILL REGRESS / on-track if none weak).

4. EMIT:
   - the per-step status table (8 rows, status 1-5),
   - the earliest weak step + its phase diagnosis,
   - the §10 per-step intervention for the earliest weak step,
   - the next-step focus (the step to build once the weak step is cleared).
```

An agent that can execute steps 1-4 from this document alone produces a per-step status,
the identified earliest weak step, and the intervention — the three outputs a Kotter
application requires.

## 13. Worked Example — ERP Rollout Across Business Functions

A fully-worked application of the 8 steps to one named transformation: an **ERP rollout
across Finance, Operations, and Customer Service**. This demonstrates the §12 procedure
and serves as the regression fixture.

### Step 2: Per-Step Status Assessment

| Step | What good looks like (this transformation) | Assessed status |
|------|--------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| **1. Urgency** | Leaders can name the concrete driver (legacy ERP end-of-life, rising support cost) and act on it | 5 — Strong |
| **2. Coalition** | A cross-functional team with CFO/COO sponsorship and IT + process leads, working as one team | 4 — Adequate |
| **3. Vision** | A clear "one integrated platform, one source of truth" picture, communicable in under 5 min, with initiatives | 4 — Adequate |
| **4. Communicate** | Town halls plus a single launch email, then little reinforcement; leaders inconsistent on the new process | 2 — Weak |
| **5. Empower** | Some legacy approval workflows still in place; a few managers still route work the old way | 3 — Borderline |
| **6. Short-term wins** | No early wins yet planned or delivered | 2 — Weak |
| **7. Consolidate** | Not yet reached | 1 — Absent |
| **8. Anchor** | Not yet reached | 1 — Absent |

### Step 3: Earliest Weak Step + Phase Diagnosis

- **Earliest weak step:** Step 4 (Communicate) at status 2 — the first step in 1→8 order
  scoring ≤3.
- **Phase diagnosis:** one of Steps 1-4 is ≤3 → **CLIMATE NOT ESTABLISHED.** Do not push
  Steps 5-8 hard. Even though Step 5 (3) and Step 6 (2) are also weak, the constraint is
  the *earliest* weak step — fixing wins (Step 6) before the vision is properly
  communicated (Step 4) produces wins no one can interpret.

### Step 4: Emit

- **Per-step status table:** produced above (8 rows, 1-5).
- **Earliest weak step:** **Step 4 — Communicate the Vision** (status 2).
- **Intervention (per §10):** saturate every channel with the vision; make leader behavior
  visibly consistent with the new process (the inconsistency is the most corrosive part of
  the Step 4 weakness here).
- **Next-step focus:** once communication is solid (Step 4 → ≥4), clear the residual
  legacy approval workflows and realign the managers still routing work the old way (Step
  5), *then* deliberately engineer the first visible win (Step 6). Steps 7-8 do not begin
  until the climate (1-4) and momentum (5-6) steps are in place.

This example is the regression fixture: an agent reading only this document must reproduce
the earliest-weak-step (Step 4), the phase diagnosis (CLIMATE NOT ESTABLISHED), and the
intervention from the §10 and §12 logic.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Signal | Root Cause | Remediation |
|-------------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Skipping straight to action** | Empowerment, restructuring, or tooling (Step 5+) begins with no urgency, coalition, or communicated vision | Treating Kotter as a project plan to execute rather than a sequence-gate; impatience to "do something" | Establish the climate first (Steps 1-4); do not push Steps 5-8 until the earliest weak climate step is cleared (§10) |
| **Under-communicating the vision by 10×** | One launch memo or town hall, then silence; the vision is not repeated | Assuming the message landed because it was sent once; underestimating saturation needs | Communicate the vision 10-100× more than feels necessary, across every channel; lead by example (§5/Step 4) |
| **Declaring victory too early** | The first short-term win (Step 6) is treated as completion; resources and urgency are withdrawn | Project mentality (deliver and close) rather than transformation mentality (sustain until durable) | Use win-credibility to drive bigger changes; do not relax until the change is anchored (§8/Step 7). See `hypercare-plan.md` for the go-live application of this rule — reference, do not restate |
| **Coalition in name only** | A named guiding team that is junior, powerless, or fractured by turf | Sponsorship treated as a title; the coalition assembled for optics, not power | Build the coalition with sufficient position power and the right skill mix, working as a real team (§3/Step 2) |
| **Manufactured (false) urgency** | Anxious, directionless activity; meeting churn presented as momentum | Confusing panic and busy-ness with genuine felt need | Build *true* urgency tied to real evidence and directed at the vision (§2/Step 1), not activity for its own sake |

## Behavioral Markers

| Dimension | Principal Behavior | Junior Behavior |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Urgency-setting** | Builds true urgency from real current evidence; raises felt need above complacency before recruiting the coalition | Manufactures panic or skips urgency entirely; mistakes busy-ness for momentum |
| **Coalition-building** | Assembles a coalition with sufficient power and the right management+leadership mix, working as one team | Accepts a junior or powerless guiding team; treats sponsorship as a title |
| **Vision communication** | Saturates every channel 10-100×; leads by example and corrects leader inconsistency visibly | Sends one memo and assumes it landed; tolerates leaders acting against the vision |
| **Sequencing discipline** | Identifies the earliest weak step and fixes it before pushing later steps; does not skip stages | Jumps to action (restructuring, tooling) without establishing the climate first |
| **Sustaining momentum** | Engineers visible early wins, then presses harder; refuses to declare victory until the change is anchored in culture | Treats the first win as the finish line; withdraws support and lets the change regress |

# Bridges Transition Model Reference

## Purpose

This reference is the authoritative source for the Bridges Transition Model
(Ending/Losing/Letting Go → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) — the **psychological-transition
layer** of the change-management methodology suite — consumed by the change-management
skill as a **selectable methodology** (routed through `methodology-selection.md`, the
cross-methodology selector) and by the sibling references. It owns the platform treatment
of the three transition zones, the per-zone leader actions, the change-vs-transition
distinction at the heart of the model, the per-zone assessment readout, the
cross-methodology selection boundary (Bridges vs ADKAR / Kotter / Lewin / 7-S), and the
unified application procedure an agent runs to lead a real change's *human transition*.

Bridges' lens is the **person's internal psychology of transition**, not the external
event of change. Its founding distinction: **change is situational and external** (the new
system goes live, the reorg takes effect, the process is replaced — a dated event);
**transition is psychological and internal** (the three-zone reorientation a person moves
through to come to terms with the new situation). Change can be scheduled; transition
cannot — it runs on the person's own clock. The model's whole claim is that *organizations
manage the change and neglect the transition*, and unmanaged transition is what makes a
well-executed change fail to take. This is distinct from — and complementary to — ADKAR's
*behavioral-state* lens (`adkar-framework.md`, which owns per-person/per-group readiness
scoring): ADKAR measures **whether** a person has reached each adoption state; Bridges
attends to the **emotional/identity passage** between states. The two are run together —
Bridges runs underneath ADKAR as the human-experience layer (see §6). The cross-methodology
selection boundary lives in §6 and feeds the selector.

## 1. The 3-Zone Model (orientation)

Bridges' model is a **passage**, not a checklist: every transition starts with an **ending**
(you cannot begin something new until you let go of the old), passes through a disorienting
**neutral zone** (the old is gone but the new is not yet operational), and arrives at a
**new beginning** (the new identity and orientation take hold). Counter-intuitively,
transition **starts with an ending** — the model's signature inversion of the everyday
assumption that change starts with the new thing. The three zones overlap and are not
strictly sequential in time (a person can be grieving one loss while exploring a new
beginning), but the *psychological work* of each zone must be done for the transition to
complete.

| Zone | What it is (1 line) | The shift it drives | Primary failure signal if neglected |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **1. Ending / Losing / Letting Go** | The person lets go of the old way, old identity, old competence | Old reality + identity → released | Resistance, grief, anger, bargaining; people cling to the old way and "wait it out" |
| **2. The Neutral Zone** | The in-between — old gone, new not yet operational or fluent | Released → disoriented, exploring | Confusion, overload, anxiety, low productivity; cynics and old-way advocates regain ground |
| **3. The New Beginning** | The new identity, orientation, and competence take hold | Disoriented → re-identified with the new | New beginning never consolidates; people comply outwardly but never internalize; reversion |

**Passage principle:** The three zones are a **psychological sequence-gate** — the work of
an earlier zone gates the later ones. A person pushed toward a New Beginning who has not
been allowed to make an Ending has nothing to begin *from*; their "new beginning" is
hollow compliance over an un-grieved loss. The constraint is always the *earliest*
incomplete zone (§5). Unlike a strict time sequence, the zones overlap — but the leader's
obligation is to support the zone-work each person most needs, and the earliest-incomplete
zone is where that support must go first.

## 2. Zone 1 — Ending / Losing / Letting Go

**What it is:** The deliberate work of helping people **let go of the old way** — the old
process, the old tools, the old identity, and the hard-won competence the change renders
obsolete. Every transition begins here. The defining insight is that what people resist is
usually **not the change itself but the loss** the change imposes — loss of competence
("I was an expert; now I'm a novice"), loss of identity, loss of relationships, loss of
control, loss of the familiar. Endings must be **named, marked, and grieved**; pretending
nothing is being lost is the fastest way to stall a transition.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Losses named explicitly | The specific losses (competence, status, relationships, routine) are identified and acknowledged out loud | "It's just a system change, nothing to grieve"; losses denied or minimized | **Identify what is ending and for whom — name the losses explicitly**, including the ones that look small from the outside but loom large to the person |
| Losses honored, not dismissed | People feel their loss is acknowledged as legitimate, not treated as resistance to be overcome | Grief and anger labeled "resistance" and pushed through; over-reassurance ("you'll love it") | **Acknowledge the loss openly and mark the ending** (a real close-out, not a celebration) — treat over-reaction as the size of the loss, not as obstruction |
| Information given repeatedly | People know what exactly is ending, what is *not* ending, and why | Ambiguity about what is changing vs staying; people fill the gap with worst-case assumptions | **Tell people what is over and what is not**; give the information again and again (people in loss do not absorb it the first time); define what they get to keep |
| The past respected | The old way is honored as having served its purpose, not denigrated | The old way is mocked or blamed; people who built it feel attacked | **Honor the past**; take a piece of the old way forward where you can; never trash what people invested in |

**Failure mode:** *Treating resistance as obstruction rather than loss.* When grief, anger,
or bargaining are labeled "resistance to change" and managed by pushing harder, the leader
attacks the symptom and inflames the cause. The person is not being difficult — they are
losing something real and have not been allowed to let go of it. Name and honor the loss
first; the "resistance" subsides when the ending is permitted.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *behavioral* Desire element (the per-person
choice to support the change) and the WIIFM/involvement interventions; Lewin's **Unfreeze**
stage and Kotter Steps 1-4 are the external-change analogues. bridges-transition.md owns the
*psychological ending* — the loss-and-grief work *underneath* a low Desire score. A group
stuck at ADKAR Desire is very often a group whose Ending has not been honored; reference
`adkar-framework.md` for the Desire scoring and barrier rule — do not restate them here.

## 3. Zone 2 — The Neutral Zone

**What it is:** The disorienting in-between — the **old is gone but the new is not yet
operational or fluent**. Bridges calls this the most critical and most neglected zone: it
is uncomfortable, low-productivity, and emotionally raw, so leaders rush people through it
or pretend it does not exist. But the neutral zone is also where **creativity, learning,
and renewal actually happen** — it is the psychological correlate of Lewin's "moving" stage
and ADKAR's Knowledge/Ability formation. The leader's job is **not to eliminate the neutral
zone but to make it survivable and productive**: normalize the discomfort, provide structure
and short-term goals so people are not lost, and protect the space rather than collapsing it
prematurely.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| The discomfort is normalized | People understand the confusion/low productivity is the *expected* in-between, not personal failure or project failure | The dip is read as "the change isn't working"; people blame themselves or the change | **Normalize the neutral zone explicitly** — name it, explain it is temporary and expected, and reframe it as the passage everyone is in |
| Temporary structures in place | Interim roles, short-term goals, and clear "what to do this week" guidance exist for the in-between | People are left to drift with no map; ambiguity becomes paralysis | **Create temporary structures and short-term, achievable goals** — give people something concrete to stand on while the new state is built |
| Two-way communication is dense | People can voice confusion and get answers fast; leaders hear what is breaking | Silence; problems accumulate unspoken; rumor fills the vacuum | **Strengthen connections and communication** — frequent check-ins, listening forums, fast answers; over-communicate in the vacuum |
| The zone is protected, not rushed | The space for learning/experimentation is held open long enough for the new way to form | Leaders collapse the zone early ("just get on with it"); learning is cut off before fluency | **Protect the neutral zone** — resist the urge to declare the transition over; treat it as the seedbed for the new way, not dead time to minimize |

**Failure mode:** *Collapsing the neutral zone prematurely* — treating the disorienting
in-between as wasted time to be compressed, pulling support, and demanding immediate
fluency. The neutral zone is where the new competence and identity are actually built;
cutting it short forces people back to the old way (the only thing they are still fluent
in) or strands them in permanent confusion. The dip is not the enemy — abandoning people
in it is.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *behavioral* Knowledge and Ability elements
and the **valley-of-despair** support-ramp rule (do not pull support at the bottom of the
dip); Lewin's **Change/Move** stage and Kotter Steps 5-7 are the external-change analogues.
bridges-transition.md owns the *psychological neutral zone* — the emotional/identity
experience of the same in-between the valley-of-despair J-curve measures in productivity
terms. The neutral zone and the valley of despair are the same disruption seen through two
lenses (psychology vs productivity); reference `adkar-framework.md` for the valley
parameters and the K/Ab scoring — do not restate them here.

## 4. Zone 3 — The New Beginning

**What it is:** The point at which people **re-identify with the new way** — the new
process, role, and competence stop being "the thing we're being made to do" and become "how
we work." A new beginning is **psychological, not procedural**: go-live is the *change*
(external, dated); the new beginning is the *transition's completion* (internal, on the
person's clock), and it arrives only after the ending has been made and the neutral zone
traversed. New beginnings cannot be forced or scheduled — but they can be **reinforced**:
Bridges' "**4 P's**" (Purpose, Picture, Plan, Part) equip people to make the beginning, and
early wins plus consistent reinforcement help it consolidate.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Purpose is clear | People understand *why* the new way matters — the meaning behind the change, not just the mechanics | The new way is mandated with no compelling "why"; people see motion without meaning | **Purpose** — explain the reason for the change compellingly; give the new beginning a meaning worth re-identifying with |
| Picture is vivid | People can *see* themselves operating in the new state — what it looks and feels like | The future is abstract; people can't picture their own place in it | **Picture** — paint a concrete image of the new state and the person's role in it |
| Plan is concrete | People have a step-by-step path into the new way, not just an endpoint | The destination is named but the path is missing; people don't know their next move | **Plan** — give a phased, specific plan for reaching and operating in the new state |
| Part is owned | Each person knows their specific part to play in the outcome | People are spectators to the change, not participants with a role | **Part** — give everyone a tangible part to play; participation is what converts compliance into ownership |
| Beginning reinforced | Early wins are celebrated; the new identity is recognized and rewarded so it consolidates | The beginning is announced then left to fend for itself; no reinforcement; quiet reversion | **Reinforce** — celebrate early wins, recognize the new behavior, and be consistent so the new beginning holds and does not slide back |

**Failure mode:** *Mistaking go-live (the change) for the new beginning (the transition's
completion).* When leaders treat the cut-over date as the finish line, they stop supporting
the transition exactly when the new beginning is most fragile — before people have
re-identified with the new way. The result is outward compliance over an un-consolidated
beginning: the moment attention shifts, people revert. The new beginning is done when the
new way is *who people are*, not when the system went live.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *behavioral* Reinforcement element
(sustaining the new behavior per person, measured past go-live) and `hypercare-plan.md`
owns the post-go-live reinforcement-check milestone; Lewin's **Refreeze** stage and Kotter
Step 8 are the external-change analogues. bridges-transition.md owns the *psychological new
beginning* — the identity re-formation *underneath* a sustained Reinforcement score. ADKAR
Reinforcement and the Bridges New Beginning meet directly (see §6): reference
`adkar-framework.md` for the Reinforcement scoring and `hypercare-plan.md` for the
reinforcement-check milestone — do not restate them here.

## 5. Sequencing & Zone-Gate Rules

The Bridges analogue of ADKAR's barrier-point gating, Kotter's earliest-weak-step rule, and
Lewin's earliest-weak-stage rule. The three zones are a **psychological sequence-gate**: the
later zone-work is undermined when the earlier zone-work is incomplete. Assess each zone on a
five-level readout — mirroring ADKAR's 1-5 scale and the Kotter/Lewin per-stage readouts to
keep the methodology suite consistent. (Because the zones overlap in time, the readout
measures *how completely the zone's psychological work has been done* for the group, not a
calendar position.)

### Per-Zone Status Readout (1-5)

| Status | Label | What it means for the zone |
|--------|-------|----------------------------|
| 5 | **Strong** | The zone's psychological work is fully done; the passage it enables is solidly in place |
| 4 | **Adequate** | Zone-work met; minor gaps that do not threaten the later zones |
| 3 | **Borderline** | Zone-work partially done; at risk under pressure; needs reinforcement |
| 2 | **Weak** | Significant gaps; the psychological work this zone requires is not reliably done |
| 1 | **Absent** | Zone-work not done; the later zones have no foundation to build on |

### Zone-Gate Logic

```
FOR the change effort (per affected group):
  Assess Ending        status 1-5   (have the losses been named, honored, let go?)
  Assess Neutral Zone  status 1-5   (is the in-between normalized, structured, supported?)
  Assess New Beginning status 1-5   (have people re-identified with the new way — 4 P's + reinforcement?)

  earliest_weak_zone = first zone, in Ending -> Neutral Zone -> New Beginning order, status <= 3

  Transition diagnosis:
    IF Ending <= 3:        ENDING NOT MADE
       -> do not push the New Beginning; there is nothing to begin from. Honor the loss
          first — name it, mark it, give the information repeatedly (§2).
    ELSE IF Neutral Zone <= 3: STUCK IN THE NEUTRAL ZONE
       -> the new way is not forming; do not declare the transition done. Normalize the
          zone, supply temporary structures and short-term goals, protect the space (§3).
    ELSE IF New Beginning <= 3: BEGINNING NOT CONSOLIDATED
       -> compliance over an un-internalized beginning; it will revert. Apply the 4 P's
          and reinforce early wins (§4) before treating the transition as complete.

  ACTION = the per-zone intervention for earliest_weak_zone (see table below)
```

**Address-the-earliest-weak-zone-first rule:** Like ADKAR's barrier point, Kotter's
earliest-weak-step, and Lewin's earliest-weak-stage, the constraint is the *earliest*
incomplete zone — investing in a later zone while an earlier one is incomplete wastes the
investment (pushing a New Beginning before the Ending is honored produces hollow compliance
over an un-grieved loss).

### Per-Zone Intervention (when each zone is the earliest weak zone)

| Earliest weak zone | Intervention before advancing |
|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Ending** | Name the specific losses and for whom; acknowledge and mark the ending openly; tell people what is over and what is not (repeatedly); honor the past — do not label grief as resistance |
| **Neutral Zone** | Normalize the in-between as expected and temporary; create temporary structures and short-term achievable goals; strengthen two-way communication; protect the zone — do not collapse it prematurely or pull support |
| **New Beginning** | Apply the 4 P's (Purpose, Picture, Plan, Part); give everyone a part to play; celebrate and reinforce early wins consistently so the new identity consolidates and does not slide back |

## 6. When to Use Bridges vs ADKAR / Kotter / Lewin / 7-S

This is the **cross-methodology selection surface** — a feed to the methodology selector
(`methodology-selection.md`, the cluster-canonical home for choosing among the change
methodologies; it reads the differentiators below to recommend a methodology for a given
change). Until that selector is consulted, this table is the Bridges-anchored view of the
boundary. The two selection axes are **unit of change** (what the methodology operates on)
and **dominant risk** (the risk it is best suited to manage). Because Bridges runs
*underneath* the structural methodologies as the human-experience layer, each row also notes
the Lewin stage it maps onto (Bridges is one of Lewin's three operationalizations — see
`lewin-3-stage.md` §1).

| Methodology | Unit of change | Temporal shape | Dominant risk it manages | Best when | Maps onto (Lewin stage) |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|--------------------------|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Bridges (Transition)** | The person's psychology (transition) | Phase/zone model (Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) | Emotional/identity disruption is mishandled; transition neglected while change is managed | Identity, loss, or emotional disruption is the dominant risk under any structural change — and the change keeps failing to "take" despite good execution | Ending → **Unfreeze** · Neutral Zone → **Change** · New Beginning → **Refreeze** |
| **ADKAR** | The individual (per person/group) | Sequence (5 elements) | A group is not individually *ready* to adopt | You need to know *whether each group is ready* and where each is blocked (behavioral states) | A,D → Unfreeze · K,Ab → Change · R → Refreeze |
| **Kotter (8-Step)** | The organization (top-down) | Sequence (8 steps) | Large transformation stalls / loses momentum | Org-wide, urgency-driven process change; you need to *lead the org through* a big transformation | Steps 1-4 → Unfreeze · 5-7 → Change · 8 → Refreeze |
| **Lewin (3-Stage)** | The change state | State model (unfreeze → change → refreeze) | Over-complicating a simple change; skipping refreeze | You need the simplest change-state frame, or the foundational map situating the others | (is the frame) |
| **7-S** | The org's seven elements | Static snapshot (no sequence) | Org elements drift out of alignment | You need to *diagnose alignment* across the seven S's before or after a change | Alignment check at any stage |

**Bridges ↔ ADKAR complementarity (they are complementary, not competing):** Bridges is the
*psychological-transition* lens (the emotional/identity passage a person moves through);
ADKAR is the *behavioral-state* lens (whether each person has reached each adoption state).
They are run together, not chosen between: ADKAR diagnoses **which state a group is stuck
in**, and Bridges attends to the **emotional passage** that gets them unstuck. The two meet
at two specific seams:

- **Desire ↔ Ending.** A group blocked at ADKAR **Desire** is, psychologically, a group
  that has not made its **Ending** — it has not been allowed to let go of the old way and
  grieve the loss. Run the Ending work (§2: name and honor the loss) to move a stuck Desire
  score. WIIFM framing alone (the ADKAR Desire intervention) lands flat on an un-grieved
  loss; the Ending is the layer underneath.
- **Reinforcement ↔ New Beginning.** ADKAR **Reinforcement** (the new behavior sustained,
  measured past go-live) and the Bridges **New Beginning** (the new identity internalized)
  are the same consolidation seen behaviorally vs psychologically. A change that is
  behaviorally reinforced but psychologically un-begun is compliance without ownership;
  apply the 4 P's (§4) so the Reinforcement rests on a real New Beginning.

The Neutral Zone, in between, is the psychological correlate of ADKAR's **Knowledge/Ability**
formation and the **valley-of-despair** dip (§3). Lewin is the frame both Bridges and ADKAR
sit inside; Kotter leads the org through the same arc top-down; 7-S checks alignment at any
point.

**Selection summary:** choose **Bridges** when *emotional/identity transition* is the
dominant risk — when people are losing competence, status, or identity, when a change keeps
failing to take despite sound execution, or when "resistance" is really un-honored grief;
choose **ADKAR** when the unit is the *individual/group* and the risk is *non-adoption*
(behavioral readiness); choose **Kotter** for a *whole-organization* transformation at risk
of *stalling*; choose **Lewin** for the simplest state frame; choose **7-S** when the need is
an *alignment diagnostic*. Methodologies compose — Bridges runs underneath whichever
structural methodology is leading the change (see `methodology-selection.md` for the
combine-methodologies guidance).

## 7. Unified Application Procedure

This is the deterministic procedure an agent executes to lead a real change's human
transition with Bridges. It ties §§1-5 into a runnable flow.

```
1. CONFIRM Bridges is the right methodology per §6:
   - the dominant risk is emotional/identity disruption (loss of competence, status,
     identity, control), OR a change keeps failing to "take" despite sound structural
     execution, OR observed "resistance" is really un-honored loss.
   (If the risk is behavioral non-adoption, route to ADKAR — and recall Bridges runs
    underneath it; if the unit is the whole org and the risk is stall, route to Kotter;
    if the need is the simplest state frame, route to Lewin; if an alignment snapshot,
    7-S. Bridges composes with all of them — see §6 / methodology-selection.md.)

2. INVENTORY the losses per affected group (the Ending work, §2):
   - for each group, name what is ending and the specific loss (competence, status,
     relationships, routine, control). This list grounds the Ending assessment.

3. ASSESS the current status of each of the 3 zones (Strong -> Absent, 1-5 per §5),
   per affected group, using the loss inventory to ground the Ending status.

4. COMPUTE the earliest weak zone per §5:
   earliest_weak_zone = first zone (Ending -> Neutral Zone -> New Beginning) with
   status <= 3, else "None". Derive the transition diagnosis (ENDING NOT MADE /
   STUCK IN THE NEUTRAL ZONE / BEGINNING NOT CONSOLIDATED / on-track if none weak).

5. EMIT:
   - the loss inventory (what is ending, for whom),
   - the per-zone status (3 rows, 1-5) per group,
   - the earliest weak zone + its transition diagnosis,
   - the §5 per-zone intervention for the earliest weak zone (lead with naming and
     honoring the loss when Ending is the constraint),
   - the next-zone focus (the zone-work to build once the weak zone is cleared).
```

An agent that can execute steps 1-5 from this document alone produces a loss inventory, a
per-zone status, the identified earliest weak zone, and the intervention — the outputs a
Bridges transition application requires.

## 8. Worked Example — Outsourcing an In-House Function

A fully-worked application of the model to one named change: **outsourcing an in-house
function** (an internal team's work is moved to a vendor; the team is redeployed to new
roles). This is a transition-heavy change — the dominant risk is loss of identity and
competence, not procedure — so Bridges is the right lens (§6). This demonstrates the §7
procedure and serves as the regression fixture.

### Step 2: Loss Inventory (the Ending work, §2)

| Affected group | What is ending | Specific losses named |
|----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| **The redeployed team** | Ownership of the function they built and ran | Competence ("I was the expert; now I'm a novice in a new role"), identity ("this work was *who I am*"), relationships (the team breaks up), control (their future is decided for them) |
| **Internal partners** | Their trusted in-house point of contact | A known relationship and fast informal service replaced by a vendor SLA and ticket queue |

### Step 3-4: Per-Zone Status + Earliest Weak Zone (the redeployed team)

| Zone | What good looks like (this change) | Assessed status |
|------|------------------------------------|-----------------|
| **Ending** | The losses above are named and honored; the team's contribution is recognized; what is over (and what they keep) is stated repeatedly | 2 — Weak (leadership announced the outsourcing as an efficiency win and never acknowledged the loss; grief is being read as "resistance") |
| **Neutral Zone** | The in-between is normalized; temporary roles and short-term goals bridge to the new assignments; communication is dense | 1 — Absent (no interim structure; the team is "waiting to hear" about new roles) |
| **New Beginning** | The team has re-identified with new roles via the 4 P's; early wins reinforced | 1 — Absent (not reached) |

- **Earliest weak zone:** **Ending** at status 2 — the first zone in Ending → Neutral Zone →
  New Beginning order scoring ≤3.
- **Transition diagnosis:** Ending ≤3 → **ENDING NOT MADE.** Pushing the New Beginning now
  (selling the team on their exciting new roles) lands on an un-grieved loss and reads as
  dismissive — it deepens the "resistance" leadership is already mislabeling. Even though the
  Neutral Zone and New Beginning are also un-started, the constraint is the *earliest* weak
  zone: the Ending must be honored before anything can begin. This also maps to a low ADKAR
  **Desire** score (§6, Desire ↔ Ending seam): the team will not *choose* the new roles until
  the loss of the old one is acknowledged.

### Step 5: Emit

- **Loss inventory:** produced above (competence, identity, relationships, control for the
  team; relationship/service loss for internal partners).
- **Per-zone status (redeployed team):** Ending 2, Neutral Zone 1, New Beginning 1.
- **Earliest weak zone:** **Ending** (status 2) → **ENDING NOT MADE.**
- **Intervention (per §5):** name the specific losses out loud and for whom; acknowledge and
  mark the ending of the function honestly (recognize what the team built — honor the past);
  tell people clearly what is over and what is *not* (and repeat it). Do **not** label the
  grief as resistance or rush to sell the new roles. (Behaviorally, this is also the move that
  unblocks the stuck ADKAR Desire score — §6.)
- **Next-zone focus:** once the Ending is honored (Ending ≥4), build the Neutral Zone —
  normalize the in-between, stand up temporary roles and short-term goals so the team is not
  "waiting to hear," and strengthen communication — *then* lead the New Beginning with the 4
  P's (Purpose/Picture/Plan/Part) and reinforce early wins in the new roles.

This example is the regression fixture: an agent reading only this document must reproduce
the earliest weak zone (Ending), the transition diagnosis (ENDING NOT MADE), the
Desire ↔ Ending mapping (§6), and the name-and-honor-the-loss intervention from the §5 and §7
logic.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Signal | Root Cause | Remediation |
|-------------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Skipping the Ending (begin-first)** | The change is launched straight into the New Beginning ("here's the exciting new way!") with no acknowledgement of what is being lost | Forgetting that transition *starts* with an ending; discomfort with naming loss; treating change (external) as if it were transition (internal) | Start with the Ending (§2) — name and honor the specific losses before selling any beginning; there is nothing to begin from until the old is let go |
| **Mislabeling grief as resistance** | Anger, bargaining, and grief in affected people are managed as "resistance to change" to be overcome by pushing harder | Reading the symptom (resistance) instead of the cause (loss); confusing emotional reaction with obstruction | Treat the over-reaction as the *size of the loss*, not as obstruction; acknowledge and honor the loss (§2) — the "resistance" subsides when the ending is permitted |
| **Collapsing the Neutral Zone** | The disorienting in-between is treated as wasted time; support is pulled and immediate fluency is demanded | Discomfort with low productivity and ambiguity; mistaking the neutral zone for dead time rather than the seedbed of the new way | Normalize and *protect* the neutral zone (§3) — supply temporary structures and short-term goals; hold the space open long enough for the new competence to form; do not pull support in the dip (see the `adkar-framework.md` valley-of-despair ramp — reference, do not restate) |
| **Go-live mistaken for the New Beginning** | The cut-over date is treated as the finish line; transition support stops at go-live | Confusing the *change* (external, dated event) with the *transition* (internal, on the person's clock); project-completion mentality | Treat go-live as the change, not the transition's completion (§4); apply the 4 P's and reinforce early wins until people have re-identified with the new way — the new beginning is psychological, not procedural |
| **The 4 P's skipped at the beginning** | A new beginning is mandated with no Purpose, no Picture, no Plan, and no Part for people to play | Assuming the new way is self-evidently good; equating announcement with enablement | Equip the beginning with all four P's (§4) — a compelling Purpose, a vivid Picture, a concrete Plan, and a real Part for each person; participation converts compliance into ownership |

## Behavioral Markers

| Dimension | Principal Behavior | Junior Behavior |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Change-vs-transition discipline** | Distinguishes the external change (dated event) from the internal transition (on the person's clock); manages both, and never mistakes go-live for the transition's completion | Treats the change event as the whole job; declares the transition done at go-live; ignores the internal passage entirely |
| **Honoring the ending** | Names the specific losses and for whom; marks and honors the ending; gives the information repeatedly; honors the past | Skips the ending and launches the new way; minimizes or denies loss; mislabels grief as resistance and pushes harder |

# Lewin 3-Stage Model Reference

## Purpose

This reference is the authoritative source for Lewin's 3-Stage change model (Unfreeze →
Change → Refreeze) and its paired diagnostic engine, **Force-Field Analysis**, consumed
by the change-management skill as a **selectable methodology** (routed through
`methodology-selection.md`, the cross-methodology selector) and by the sibling
references. It owns the platform treatment of the three stages, their sequence-gating,
the Force-Field Analysis scoring scale and the net-force decision rule (Lewin's analogue
of ADKAR's barrier point), the per-stage assessment readout, the foundational-meta-frame
mapping that situates the other methodologies inside Lewin's three stages, and the
unified application procedure an agent runs to apply Lewin to a real change.

Lewin's lens is the **change state itself**: it answers "what state is this change in —
are we loosening the old equilibrium, moving to the new one, or locking the new one in?"
at the simplest useful altitude. This is distinct from — and the frame underneath —
ADKAR's *individual-adoption* lens (`adkar-framework.md`, per-person/per-group readiness)
and Kotter's *organizational-process* lens (`kotter-8-step.md`, leading the whole org
through a transformation). Lewin is the **foundational meta-frame**: the other change
methodologies operationalize one or more of its three stages rather than competing with
it. The foundational-meta-frame mapping lives in §1 and the cross-methodology selection
boundary in §11; both feed the selector.

## 1. The 3-Stage Model (orientation) — the foundational meta-frame

Lewin's model treats organizational behavior as a **quasi-stationary equilibrium**: the
current state is not static but held in place by a balance of opposing forces (the
Force-Field, §4). Changing it is a three-stage state transition — first destabilize the
equilibrium, then move to the new state, then re-stabilize at the new equilibrium so it
does not snap back. The model's whole claim is that *all three stages are required*:
a change driven without unfreezing meets the full weight of the restraining forces, and
a change that moves but never refreezes regresses to the old equilibrium the moment
attention shifts.

| Stage | What it is (1 line) | The shift it drives | Primary failure signal if skipped or weak |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Unfreeze** | Destabilize the current equilibrium; create readiness and felt need to change | Status-quo stability → openness to change | The change meets the full restraining force; resistance overwhelms it; "why change?" inertia |
| **Change (Move/Transition)** | Move to the new state — new processes, behaviors, structures — while the field is unfrozen | Old way → new way (in motion) | Movement without unfreezing stalls; people comply briefly then revert; confusion in the transition |
| **Refreeze** | Re-stabilize at the new equilibrium so the change becomes the new normal | New way (fragile) → new way (the default) | Change reverts to the old equilibrium; gains are temporary; "we tried that, it didn't stick" |

**Sequence principle:** The three stages are a **sequence-gate** — each stage establishes
the condition the next depends on. Moving before unfreezing wastes the move (it is fought
by the full restraining force); refreezing before the move is complete locks in a
half-change. The constraint is always the *earliest* incomplete stage (§10).

### Lewin as the foundational meta-frame (the meta-frame mapping)

Lewin is the **frame the other methodologies sit inside**: each operationalizes one or
more of the three stages at finer grain or for a different unit of change. Run Lewin when
the simplest change-state frame is what the situation needs; run the others *within* its
stages when their finer lens is warranted (route via §11 / `methodology-selection.md`).

| Methodology | Unit it operates on | Maps onto Lewin's stages | What it adds inside the frame |
|-------------|---------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Lewin (this doc)** | The change state | (is the frame) Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze | The meta-frame itself + the Force-Field diagnostic (§4) |
| **Kotter (8-Step)** | The organization (top-down) | Steps 1-4 ≈ **Unfreeze** · Steps 5-7 ≈ **Change** · Step 8 ≈ **Refreeze** | The org-level leadership sequence that drives each stage |
| **ADKAR** | The individual (per person/group) | A+D ≈ **Unfreeze** · K+Ab ≈ **Change** · R ≈ **Refreeze** | The per-person readiness scoring underneath each stage |
| **Bridges (Transition)** | The person's psychology | Ending ≈ **Unfreeze** · Neutral Zone ≈ **Change** · New Beginning ≈ **Refreeze** | The human-experience/emotional layer running underneath |
| **7-S** | The org's seven elements | Diagnoses alignment **at any stage** (static snapshot) | An alignment check applied before, during, or after any stage |

This mapping is the spine of the methodology suite: Kotter, ADKAR, and Bridges are three
different-altitude operationalizations of the *same* Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze arc,
and 7-S is the alignment snapshot taken at any point along it. When in doubt about which
methodology a given change needs, locate the change in Lewin's three stages first, then
pick the finer lens for the stage that carries the dominant risk (§11).

## 2. Stage 1 — Unfreeze

**What it is:** Destabilize the current equilibrium so the organization becomes ready to
change. Unfreezing is the deliberate work of raising the driving forces and lowering the
restraining forces (§4) until the balance tips toward change — surfacing the dissatisfaction
with the status quo, making the case for change, and reducing the fear and inertia that
hold the old state in place. Nothing durable moves until the field is unfrozen.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Felt need / dissatisfaction with status quo | People can name a concrete reason the current state is no longer acceptable | Comfort with the current state; "we've always done it this way" | Surface real evidence the status quo is costlier than change; make the cost of inaction visible |
| Restraining forces lowered | The fears, habits, and structural blockers holding the old state are named and being reduced | Resistance treated as something to push through rather than reduce | Identify and lower restraining forces (§4) — reducing a restraint is more durable than adding pressure |
| Readiness, not just awareness | The organization is psychologically ready to let go of the old way | People understand the change but are not willing to release the old state | Address loss directly; involve people in shaping the change; build psychological safety |

**Failure mode:** *Driving the change without unfreezing* — adding pressure (mandates,
deadlines) while the restraining forces are untouched. The forces rebalance the instant
the pressure relents, and the added pressure often *raises* the restraining forces
(reactance). Unfreeze by lowering restraints, not only by raising drivers.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *individual* Awareness and Desire elements
(the per-person readiness that constitutes unfreezing) and the barrier-point rule; Kotter
Steps 1-4 (urgency, coalition, vision, communication) are the org-level unfreeze.
lewin-3-stage.md owns the *state-level unfreeze concept and the Force-Field mechanism*;
reference `adkar-framework.md` for the A/D scoring and `kotter-8-step.md` for the
org-level sequence — do not restate them here.

## 3. Stage 2 — Change (Move / Transition)

**What it is:** Move the organization to the new state while the field is unfrozen — new
processes, behaviors, structures, and systems. This is the transition period: the old way
is being left behind and the new way is not yet fluent. It is inherently unstable and
uncomfortable (the productivity dip lives here), which is why it must be actively
supported and must not be rushed or left to drift.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Movement is supported, not announced | People are actively guided into the new way with support, training, and coaching | The new way is mandated by memo; people are left to figure it out | Supply training, coaching, and visible support throughout the transition; expect and absorb the dip |
| Direction is clear | People know specifically what the new state looks like and how to operate in it | Ambiguity about the target state; competing versions of "new" | Make the new state concrete and consistent; remove conflicting old-way paths |
| Short feedback loops | Problems in the transition are surfaced and corrected quickly | Problems accumulate silently; people quietly revert to the old way | Run tight feedback loops; correct course fast; keep the old way genuinely unavailable |

**Failure mode:** *Treating the move as an event rather than a transition* — expecting the
new state to be reached the day it is announced. The move is a period of supported
instability; pulling support during it (or leaving old-way paths open) lets the
half-moved organization slide back before it ever reaches the new equilibrium.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *individual* Knowledge and Ability
elements (the per-person capability the move requires) and the valley-of-despair
support-ramp rule; Kotter Steps 5-7 (empower, wins, consolidate) are the org-level move.
lewin-3-stage.md owns the *state-level move concept*; reference `adkar-framework.md` for
the K/Ab scoring and the valley-of-despair ramp, and `kotter-8-step.md` for the org
sequence — do not restate them here.

## 4. Force-Field Analysis — CANONICAL (the scored driving/restraining engine)

Force-Field Analysis is Lewin's diagnostic engine and the **barrier-point analogue** for
this methodology: it makes the quasi-stationary equilibrium operational by enumerating and
**scoring** the forces *driving* the change and the forces *restraining* it, then deriving
whether — and how — the field will move. Where ADKAR computes a barrier point and Kotter
an earliest-weak-step, Lewin computes a **net force** and identifies the **dominant
restraining force** to target. This is the section a Lewin assessment reads from.

### Force Strength Scale (1-5) — CANONICAL

Score each driving force and each restraining force 1-5 on the same scale, grounded in
observable evidence of the force's real weight on the change (not its theoretical
importance).

| Score | Label | Observable Indicators (level) |
|-------|-------|-------------------------------|
| 5 | **Very Strong** | Dominant, decisive force; shapes behavior on its own; impossible to ignore |
| 4 | **Strong** | Substantial, consistently-felt force; materially shifts the balance |
| 3 | **Moderate** | Real but not decisive; felt under some conditions, not all |
| 2 | **Weak** | Minor force; occasionally felt; easily outweighed |
| 1 | **Negligible** | Present but immaterial to the outcome |

### Net-Force Decision Rule — CANONICAL

```
ENUMERATE the forces for the change:
  driving forces    = forces pushing toward the new state   (score each 1-5)
  restraining forces = forces holding the current state      (score each 1-5)

  driving_total    = sum(driving force scores)
  restraining_total = sum(restraining force scores)
  net_force        = driving_total - restraining_total

  dominant_restraint = the highest-scored restraining force
                       (ties: the one most amenable to being lowered)

  IF net_force <= 0:
    STATE = WILL NOT MOVE (or will snap back)
    ACTION = LOWER the dominant restraining force first (preferred),
             then raise driving forces as needed to make net_force > 0
  ELSE IF net_force > 0 but any single restraining force >= 4:
    STATE = WILL MOVE BUT FRAGILE (a strong restraint will reassert in Refreeze)
    ACTION = LOWER the dominant restraining force before/while moving;
             do not rely on driver pressure alone
  ELSE (net_force > 0 and no restraining force >= 4):
    STATE = READY TO MOVE
    ACTION = proceed to the Move stage with the support plan (§3)
```

**Lower-the-restraint-first rule (CANONICAL):** When net force is non-positive or a strong
restraint remains, *lower the dominant restraining force before raising driving forces.*
Adding driving pressure against an unlowered restraint increases tension on both sides —
the system becomes more stressed and more likely to snap back when the pressure relents
(the reactance dynamic). Reducing the dominant restraint moves the equilibrium durably and
at lower cost. This is the single most important judgment in a Force-Field Analysis and the
most common place a junior analyst goes wrong (see Anti-Patterns).

**Force-Field ↔ Stage mapping:** Force-Field Analysis is primarily an **Unfreeze-stage**
tool (it diagnoses what must be unfrozen and which restraint to lower), but it is re-run at
**Refreeze** to confirm the new equilibrium holds — i.e., that no strong restraining force
survives to pull the change back. A change that moved on driver pressure alone, with a
score-4+ restraint never lowered, will show that restraint reasserting in the Refreeze
re-run.

## 5. Stage 3 — Refreeze

**What it is:** Re-stabilize the organization at the new equilibrium so the change becomes
"the way we work here" and does not revert. Refreezing makes the new state the default —
embedding it in structures, processes, incentives, norms, and habits so the balance of
forces now holds the *new* state in place. The change is not done when the move completes;
it is done when the new equilibrium is self-sustaining.

| Sub-element | Observable "done" | Failure signal | Leader action |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|---------------|
| New state embedded in systems | Structures, processes, and incentives now reward and reinforce the new way | The new behavior depends on continuous effort/reminders to persist | Embed the change in process, policy, tooling, and incentives so it is the path of least resistance |
| Old equilibrium foreclosed | The old way is genuinely unavailable / no longer rewarded | Old-way paths and incentives still exist; people drift back | Remove the old-way affordances and any incentive that still rewards the old state |
| New equilibrium confirmed (Force-Field re-run) | A re-run Force-Field shows no strong restraint surviving to pull the change back | Assuming the change is stable without checking the force balance | Re-run Force-Field Analysis (§4); confirm no score-4+ restraint remains; address any that does |

**Failure mode:** *Declaring the change done at the end of the move* — skipping Refreeze
entirely. The new state is at its most fragile immediately after the move; without
embedding it in systems and foreclosing the old equilibrium, the original restraining
forces (never lowered, only out-pushed) pull the organization back. "We tried that, it
didn't stick" is almost always an unfrozen-and-moved-but-never-refrozen change.

**Composes with:** `adkar-framework.md` owns the *individual* Reinforcement element
(sustaining the new behavior per person, measured past go-live) and `hypercare-plan.md`
owns the post-go-live reinforcement-check milestone and valley parameters; Kotter Step 8
(anchor in culture) is the org-level refreeze. lewin-3-stage.md owns the *state-level
refreeze concept and the Force-Field re-run check*; reference `adkar-framework.md` for the
Reinforcement scoring and `hypercare-plan.md` for the reinforcement-check milestone — do
not restate them here.

## 6. Sequencing & Stage-Gate Rules

The Lewin analogue of ADKAR's barrier-point gating and Kotter's earliest-weak-step rule.
The three stages are a **sequence-gate**: a later stage is undermined when an earlier one
is incomplete. Assess each stage on a five-level readout — mirroring ADKAR's 1-5 scale and
Kotter's per-step readout to keep the methodology suite consistent.

### Per-Stage Status Readout (1-5)

| Status | Label | What it means for the stage |
|--------|-------|-----------------------------|
| 5 | **Strong** | Stage fully achieved; the condition it establishes is solidly in place |
| 4 | **Adequate** | Stage met; minor gaps that do not threaten downstream stages |
| 3 | **Borderline** | Stage partially done; at risk under pressure; needs reinforcement |
| 2 | **Weak** | Significant gaps; the condition this stage establishes is not reliably present |
| 1 | **Absent** | Stage not done; downstream stages have no foundation to build on |

### Sequence-Gate Logic

```
FOR the change effort:
  Assess Unfreeze   status 1-5   (is the field unfrozen? — confirm via Force-Field §4)
  Assess Change     status 1-5   (has the move to the new state happened, with support?)
  Assess Refreeze   status 1-5   (is the new equilibrium embedded and confirmed?)

  earliest_weak_stage = first stage, in Unfreeze -> Change -> Refreeze order, status <= 3

  State diagnosis:
    IF Unfreeze <= 3:   NOT READY TO MOVE
       -> do not push the Change stage; the move will be fought by the full restraining
          force. Unfreeze first — lower the dominant restraint (§4).
    ELSE IF Change <= 3: MOVE INCOMPLETE
       -> the new state is not reached; do not refreeze a half-change. Complete the
          supported move (§3) before embedding anything.
    ELSE IF Refreeze <= 3: WILL REGRESS
       -> the change is unstable and will snap back; embed it in systems and re-run
          Force-Field (§5) before treating it as done.

  ACTION = the per-stage intervention for earliest_weak_stage (see table below)
```

**Address-the-earliest-weak-stage-first rule:** Like ADKAR's barrier point and Kotter's
earliest-weak-step, the constraint is the *earliest* incomplete stage — investing in a
later stage while an earlier one is weak wastes the investment (refreezing a change that
has not finished moving locks in a half-change).

### Per-Stage Intervention (when each stage is the earliest weak stage)

| Earliest weak stage | Intervention before advancing |
|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Unfreeze** | Run Force-Field Analysis (§4); lower the dominant restraining force; raise felt need until net_force > 0 |
| **Change** | Supply training/coaching/support through the transition; make the target state concrete; keep old-way paths foreclosed; absorb the dip without pulling support |
| **Refreeze** | Embed the new state in structures/incentives/norms; foreclose the old equilibrium; re-run Force-Field to confirm no strong restraint survives |

## 7. When to Use Lewin vs ADKAR / Kotter / Bridges / 7-S

This is the **cross-methodology selection surface** — a feed to the methodology selector
(`methodology-selection.md`, the cluster-canonical home for choosing among the change
methodologies; it reads the differentiators below to recommend a methodology for a given
change). Until that selector is consulted, this table is the Lewin-anchored view of the
boundary. The two selection axes are **unit of change** (what the methodology operates on)
and **dominant risk** (the risk it is best suited to manage). Because Lewin is the
foundational meta-frame (§1), the other rows also state *which Lewin stage(s) they
operationalize*.

| Methodology | Unit of change | Temporal shape | Dominant risk it manages | Best when | Operationalizes (Lewin stage) |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|--------------------------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| **Lewin (3-Stage)** | The change state | State model (unfreeze → change → refreeze) | Over-complicating a simple change; skipping refreeze | You need the simplest change-state frame, or a foundational map to situate the other methods | (is the frame) |
| **Kotter (8-Step)** | The organization (top-down) | Sequence (8 steps) | Large transformation stalls / loses momentum | Org-wide, urgency-driven process change; you need to *lead the org through* a big transformation | Unfreeze (1-4) · Change (5-7) · Refreeze (8) |
| **ADKAR** | The individual (per person/group) | Sequence (5 elements) | A group is not individually ready to adopt | You need to know *whether each group is ready* and where each is blocked | Unfreeze (A,D) · Change (K,Ab) · Refreeze (R) |
| **Bridges (Transition)** | The person's psychology | Phase model (Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) | Emotional/identity disruption is mishandled | Identity or emotional disruption is the dominant risk under any structural change | Unfreeze (Ending) · Change (Neutral Zone) · Refreeze (New Beginning) |
| **7-S** | The org's seven elements | Static snapshot (no sequence) | Org elements drift out of alignment | You need to *diagnose alignment* across the seven S's before or after a change | Alignment check at any stage |

**Lewin as the frame the others sit inside (they are complementary, not competing):**
Lewin is not one option among five — it is the *meta-frame* the other four operationalize
(§1). Kotter leads the *organization* through Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze; ADKAR confirms
each *individual* crosses the same three stages; Bridges handles the *emotional* experience
of the same arc; 7-S checks *alignment* at any point along it. Run Lewin when the simplest
state frame is sufficient or as the orientation that tells you which finer lens a given
change needs.

**Selection summary:** choose **Lewin** when the unit of change is the *change state
itself* and the need is the *simplest frame* (or a foundational map to place the other
methods); choose **Kotter** for a *whole-organization* transformation at risk of
*stalling*; choose **ADKAR** when the unit is the *individual/group* and the risk is
*non-adoption*; choose **Bridges** when *emotional transition* is the risk; choose **7-S**
when the need is an *alignment diagnostic* rather than a change sequence. Methodologies
compose — the suite's default for a major change is to use Lewin as the frame and run the
finer lenses inside its stages (see `methodology-selection.md` for the combine-methodologies
guidance).

## 8. Unified Application Procedure

This is the deterministic procedure an agent executes to apply Lewin to a real change. It
ties §§1-6 into a runnable flow.

```
1. CONFIRM Lewin is the right methodology per §7:
   - the need is the simplest change-state frame, OR a foundational map to situate the
     other methodologies, OR an explicit user choice of Lewin.
   (If the unit is the whole org and the risk is stall, route to Kotter; if the unit is
    the individual/group and the risk is non-adoption, route to ADKAR; if emotional
    transition is the risk, route to Bridges; if the need is an alignment snapshot, route
    to 7-S. See §7 / methodology-selection.md.)

2. RUN Force-Field Analysis per §4:
   - enumerate driving and restraining forces; score each 1-5;
   - compute driving_total, restraining_total, net_force = driving_total - restraining_total;
   - identify the dominant restraining force.

3. ASSESS the current status of each of the 3 stages (Strong -> Absent, 1-5 per §6),
   using the Force-Field result to ground the Unfreeze status.

4. COMPUTE the earliest weak stage per §6:
   earliest_weak_stage = first stage (Unfreeze -> Change -> Refreeze) with status <= 3,
   else "None". Derive the state diagnosis (NOT READY TO MOVE / MOVE INCOMPLETE /
   WILL REGRESS / on-track if none weak).

5. EMIT:
   - the Force-Field table (driving vs restraining, scores, net_force, dominant restraint),
   - the per-stage status (3 rows, 1-5),
   - the earliest weak stage + its state diagnosis,
   - the §6 per-stage intervention for the earliest weak stage (lead with lowering the
     dominant restraint when Unfreeze is the constraint),
   - the next-stage focus (the stage to build once the weak stage is cleared).
```

An agent that can execute steps 1-5 from this document alone produces a scored Force-Field,
a per-stage status, the identified earliest weak stage, and the intervention — the outputs
a Lewin application requires.

## 9. Worked Example — Warehouse Scanning Process Change

A fully-worked application of the model to one named change: replacing a **paper-based
warehouse pick process with a handheld-scanner process**. This demonstrates the §8
procedure and serves as the regression fixture.

### Step 2: Force-Field Analysis (per §4)

| Driving forces (toward scanners) | Score | | Restraining forces (holding paper) | Score |
|----------------------------------|-------|---|------------------------------------|-------|
| Pick-error cost is high and visible | 5 | | Fear of being measured/timed by the device | 5 |
| Scanners are faster once learned | 4 | | Habit + muscle memory of the paper flow | 4 |
| Leadership mandate + budget committed | 4 | | Low device confidence among senior pickers | 3 |
| Real-time inventory accuracy unlocked | 3 | | Shift disruption during the learning curve | 2 |
| **driving_total** | **16** | | **restraining_total** | **14** |

- **net_force** = 16 − 14 = **+2** (positive, but thin).
- **dominant_restraint** = "Fear of being measured/timed" (score 5).
- **State (per §4):** net_force > 0 but a single restraining force = 5 (≥4) →
  **WILL MOVE BUT FRAGILE.** A score-5 restraint will reassert in Refreeze if untouched.
- **Action:** *lower the dominant restraint first* — decouple the scanner rollout from
  individual performance surveillance, communicate that explicitly, and involve senior
  pickers in the rollout — before relying on the mandate (a driver) to carry the move.

### Step 3-4: Per-Stage Status + Earliest Weak Stage

| Stage | What good looks like (this change) | Assessed status |
|-------|------------------------------------|-----------------|
| **Unfreeze** | The fear restraint is being lowered; pickers see the error-cost case and are ready to let go of paper | 2 — Weak (the score-5 fear restraint is untouched; readiness is low) |
| **Change** | Pickers are trained and supported onto the scanners with the dip absorbed | 1 — Absent (not started) |
| **Refreeze** | Scanner flow embedded; paper foreclosed; Force-Field re-run shows no strong restraint | 1 — Absent (not reached) |

- **Earliest weak stage:** **Unfreeze** at status 2 — the first stage in order scoring ≤3.
- **State diagnosis:** Unfreeze ≤3 → **NOT READY TO MOVE.** Pushing the Change stage now
  (rolling out scanners under the mandate) meets the full score-5 fear restraint; pickers
  comply while watched and revert to paper otherwise. Even though Change and Refreeze are
  also un-started, the constraint is the *earliest* weak stage — unfreeze before moving.

### Step 5: Emit

- **Force-Field table:** produced above (net_force = +2; dominant restraint = fear of
  surveillance, score 5).
- **Per-stage status:** Unfreeze 2, Change 1, Refreeze 1.
- **Earliest weak stage:** **Unfreeze** (status 2) → **NOT READY TO MOVE.**
- **Intervention (per §6):** lower the dominant restraint — decouple the rollout from
  individual surveillance and say so plainly; involve senior pickers; raise the felt
  error-cost case until readiness rises. Do **not** lead with the mandate (raising a
  driver against an unlowered score-5 restraint raises tension and reactance).
- **Next-stage focus:** once Unfreeze ≥4 (fear restraint lowered, pickers ready), run the
  supported move (§3) — train, coach, absorb the dip, foreclose paper — then Refreeze by
  embedding the scanner flow and re-running Force-Field to confirm the fear restraint has
  not reasserted.

This example is the regression fixture: an agent reading only this document must reproduce
the Force-Field net_force (+2), the dominant restraint (fear/surveillance, 5), the earliest
weak stage (Unfreeze), the state diagnosis (NOT READY TO MOVE), and the lower-the-restraint-first
intervention from the §4, §6, and §8 logic.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Signal | Root Cause | Remediation |
|-------------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Raising drivers against an unlowered restraint** | More pressure (mandates, deadlines, incentives to comply) is added while the dominant restraining force is untouched | Treating change as a push problem rather than an equilibrium to rebalance; driving forces are more visible/actionable than restraints | Run Force-Field (§4); *lower the dominant restraining force first*; add driver pressure only after the strongest restraint is reduced |
| **Skipping Unfreeze (move-first)** | The change is rolled out (Change stage) with no readiness work; resistance is "managed" reactively | Impatience to show movement; assuming a mandate substitutes for readiness | Unfreeze before moving — confirm net_force > 0 with no score-4+ restraint surviving (§4) before pushing the Change stage (§6) |
| **Skipping Refreeze (declaring done at the move)** | The effort ends when the new process is live; no embedding, no old-way foreclosure, no Force-Field re-run | Project mentality (deliver and close) rather than equilibrium mentality (re-stabilize until self-sustaining) | Treat the move's completion as the *start* of Refreeze (§5); embed in systems, foreclose the old way, re-run Force-Field to confirm no strong restraint remains |
| **Force-Field as a brainstorm list, not a scored diagnostic** | Driving and restraining forces are listed but not scored; no net_force, no dominant restraint identified | Using the visual as a discussion prop rather than the §4 decision engine | Score every force 1-5 (§4); compute net_force; identify and target the dominant restraint; let the scored result drive the action |
| **Over-applying Lewin to a change needing a finer lens** | Lewin's three stages are used where the real risk is individual non-adoption or emotional disruption, and the simple frame misses it | Defaulting to the meta-frame without checking whether a finer methodology fits the dominant risk | Use §7 / methodology-selection.md — Lewin frames the arc, but route to ADKAR (non-adoption), Kotter (org stall), or Bridges (emotional) when one of those is the dominant risk |

## Behavioral Markers

| Dimension | Principal Behavior | Junior Behavior |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Force-Field rigor** | Enumerates and *scores* forces 1-5; computes net_force; names the dominant restraint and targets it | Lists forces without scoring; treats the diagram as a brainstorm; never identifies which restraint to lower |
| **Lower-the-restraint-first discipline** | Lowers the dominant restraining force before raising driver pressure; explains why pushing harder backfires | Adds mandates/deadlines against an unlowered restraint; mistakes more pressure for more progress |
| **Stage-sequence discipline** | Identifies the earliest weak stage and fixes it first; will not refreeze a half-moved change | Jumps to the move without unfreezing, or declares victory at the move without refreezing |
| **Refreeze rigor** | Embeds the change in systems, forecloses the old equilibrium, and re-runs Force-Field to confirm stability | Treats go-live as completion; never checks whether a strong restraint survived to pull the change back |
| **Meta-frame judgment** | Uses Lewin to situate the change, then routes to the finer methodology the dominant risk warrants (§7) | Applies the simple three-stage frame to every change regardless of the real dominant risk |

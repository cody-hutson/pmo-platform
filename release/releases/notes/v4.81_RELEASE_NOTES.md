# Governance promises are now measured when a release closes

2026-10-30 · v4.81

When a release promises a governance rule that no automated check enforces, its close now counts how far the promise was kept, and any shortfall has to name the open work item that will finish it. Plan reviews, bundling and the deploy-time check gain three more checks of the same kind, and there is nothing you need to do.

> **Skip the rest** unless you run a release's plan review or its close, bundle work items into a release, or read the warnings the deploy-time check prints.

## Who this affects

- Operators who run a release's plan review or its close
- Anyone who bundles work items into a release
- Operators who read the deploy-time check's warnings, or who decide when a logged check starts blocking

## What changed for everyone using the platform

- **A release's governance promises are measured at its close.** When a release adds a governance rule that no automated check enforces, it now states which items the rule covers and how to count them, and its close records how many of those items meet the rule. A shortfall closes only with an open work item outside the release whose description or comments name the gap, and every outcome a release promises names the work item that owns it. *Why it matters:* a promise can no longer ship at zero and close as done with nobody carrying the gap.

- **Plan reviews accept a safeguard only once it has been seen working where it runs.** When a risk at plan review rests on a check, a guard or a deploy step, the review now records which risks rest on one and, for each, an independent observation of that safeguard acting on the path it protects. *Why it matters:* a risk is no longer accepted on the strength of a safeguard that has never fired where it is supposed to.

- **A work item can be bundled into a release only when its Documentation Impact section can be read.** At bundling, a work item whose Documentation Impact section the close-out reader cannot grade is held back until the section is fixed, and a work item that is already bundled gets the same reading as advice. Triage shows the same reading earlier, as advice only. *Why it matters:* a release no longer finds out at its close, after the work is done, that a work item never said which documents it changes.

- **The changelog, the release digest and the release note must agree on a release's close-out date.** A new deploy-time check compares the three for every release since that date was declared, and it already reports three older releases where they differ. The check is logged but not blocking. *Why it matters:* a release's close-out date reads the same wherever you look it up, and a disagreement is reported instead of waiting to be found by hand.

## Known limits

- This release's own close measured both promises it made, and both are short of their targets, so each is carried by a named open work item: safeguards seen working at plan review, 5 of 7 ([#7841](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/7841)), and bundled work items whose Documentation Impact section can be read, 46 of 222 ([#8114](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/8114)).
- The close-out measurement and the date-agreement check are logged but not blocking for now. The close-out check of each work item's Documentation Impact also stays logged but not blocking until most bundled work items can be read; that is tracked on [#8114](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/8114).
- The close-out measurement grades the recorded count and does not count again itself, and a governance rule that was never written into the platform's register of rules is invisible to it.
- A plan review can still accept a risk as a named residual without its safeguard, as this release's own review did for two risks whose safeguards first run after the merge.
- The three older releases the date-agreement check reports, v4.24, v4.43 and v4.45, are real disagreements left in place, because a note's date is also part of its published release page; their fix is owned by [#4327](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/4327).
- Nothing needs installing: every change runs from the repository, and no file is copied to an installed workspace. The platform's recorded version moves to v4.81 when this close lands on the main line.

Report issues at https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues, and report a suspected vulnerability privately as the [Security Policy](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/blob/main/SECURITY.md) describes.

## Reversibility

CHEAP / HIGH. Reverting the single release merge restores every check, template and procedure as it was; the new close-out and deploy-time checks are logged but not blocking, so a revert leaves nothing waiting on them, and the bundling check simply stops holding work items back. The version tag is kept as the record that the version was claimed, and the decision record ratified at this close stays in history as written.

### Operator and engineering detail

**The close measurement (#5286, ADR-228).** Gate 13 gains criterion G-CL10, run at Stage 13 Phase A14 by `deploy.sh --check-doc-gates --gate g-cl10` (`--list` first) through the existing close-gate engine, which stays offline while the wrapper does every GitHub read. Arm (i): every gate-coverage register row whose Surface cell leads with `prose` that the release added or modified declares its population in-cell, and the release plan's Verification Evidence carries a `#### Population record — <name>` block — the population-record labels of `gate-efficacy-standard.md` Requirement (c), then `conforming:` and `owner:` — bound to that declaration by its probe string and a pin containing the release head. A record below its threshold passes only with an owner that is an open issue of this repository, outside the release, whose body or comments name the population, and the Stage 13 spoke posts the record there. No obligation may be declared unmeasurable: a population no instrument reaches is enumerated by hand census. Arm (ii): the Release Outcome Statement template gains a required `**Limb owners:**` line. The gate is warn-mode, with rows under `check: "g-cl10"` and the sentinel `.github/g-cl10.enforce`. A population declaration and a limb-owner line look like this; both are this release's own:

```text
**Population:** `doc-impact-gradable-in-bundled` — population: open issues labelled `status: bundled` to which G3-20 applies per its applies-to cells and its kind-form disposition in `core/schemas/gate-criteria-spec.md`, each conforming when its parsed `doc_impact` reads `gradable`; probe: `python3 release/tools/bundle-issues-parser.py --label "status: bundled" --doc-impact-population`; threshold: 100%

**Limb owners:** measured at close → #5286 · observed acting at Stage 9 → #4919 · Documentation Impact checked upstream → #5062 · close-out dates agree → #4417.
```

**Observed acting at Stage 9 (#4919).** `review-discipline-principles.md` § 8 names the accepts-artifact-as-action class and gains PV-2f, which states what observing a control acting takes. Stage 9 Phase A4 records one `**Phase A4 control census:**` line classing every register risk `control` or `owner-act`, and one `**Phase A4 observed-acting:**` line per `control` id whose `via` is an independent observation; the refined G-PR4 reads them. An unobserved control-backed disposition is a NO-GO recommendation input the operator weighs at Phase C, never an evaluator hold. This release's own Stage 9 was the first worked example: 7 control-backed risks, 5 accepted on their observed controls and 2 accepted as residuals.

**Documentation Impact at bundling (#5062).** Gate 3 criterion G3-20 reads each candidate's Documentation Impact through the one grammar G-CL8 grades at close, via `bundle-issues-parser.py`. It blocks a card that is not yet bundled, is advisory on a re-bundle and for a kind-form card, and withholds its verdict once for the batch when the engine is unavailable; Triage Phase A4.9 reads it early, advisory. `--doc-impact-population` prints every open bundled card with its reading. The G-CL8 flip to enforce was deferred at this close, with its rationale recorded in the plan: the `g-cl8` warn rows read 8, under the drain threshold of 10, but only 46 of the 222 bundled cards G3-20 applies to are gradable, so enforcing now would fail later closes on legacy declaration debt rather than on new omissions. It is revisited when `doc-impact-gradable-in-bundled` nears its 100% threshold.

**Close-out date agreement (#4417).** `deploy.sh` Check 82 (`closeout-anchor-consistency`) runs `generate_release_index.py --verify-closeout-anchors`, which compares the close-out date on the DIGEST heading, the CHANGELOG heading and the note's frontmatter `date:` three ways for every `VERIFIED` RELEASE_LOG row from the anchor's declaration on. It is warn-mode on its own dial, with its sink observed written and its deadline armed. Its three resident findings, v4.24, v4.43 and v4.45, are true positives of one note-date writer defect, owned by #4327.

**How it shipped.** Four cards, built serially on one branch, with three Stage 6 fix passes: one after Stage 7 and two after Stage 8. Every card's final Stage 8 verdict was ACCEPT, and Stage 9 returned GO with no conditions. The release merged at `e65cf3e7` and claimed `v4.81` with a signed annotated tag on the first attempt. Stage 13 measured both declared obligations, posted each record on its owning work item, and ratified ADR-228 `Accepted` on the operator's per-record authorization.

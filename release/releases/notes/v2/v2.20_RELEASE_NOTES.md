# Project fields now manage their own lifecycle, and the platform keeps one tidy catalog

## What changed for everyone

The platform's records — tickets, artifacts, projects — are made of fields. This release gives those fields a managed lifecycle, makes the changes happen automatically, and tidies how the platform keeps track of its own moving parts.

- **Fields now have rules for who can change what, and when.** A new permission matrix says which assistant may write which field, for which kind of record, at which stage of the work — and the master "who can write what" table is lined up with it, so there is one answer instead of two that can drift apart. *Why it matters:* a field can't be quietly changed by the wrong actor at the wrong time, and the rules are written down in one place.
- **Those changes now happen automatically as work moves through its stages.** When a record advances, the assistants move its fields to the right state on their own (and across shared and portfolio-level records, not just single projects) instead of someone remembering to do it by hand. *Why it matters:* the state of your work stays current without manual bookkeeping.
- **Artifact status is unified onto one model.** Generated artifacts used to track their status in a slightly different way from everything else; that is now reconciled onto the one canonical lifecycle the rest of the platform uses (with a small extra marker for the "draft → promoted" step). The tools that create and check artifacts were moved onto the unified model. *Why it matters:* one consistent vocabulary for "what state is this in", across every kind of record.
- **The platform keeps one catalog of all its skills, plus a register of each project's artifacts.** The skill registry is evolved into a single catalog where every deployed skill is a tracked item with its lifecycle state, dependencies, and owner — and there's a new per-project Artifact Register template. *Why it matters:* you can see, in one place, what the platform is made of and what each project has produced.
- **A new sweep flags work that has gone quiet or decisions that are overdue.** The delivery engine now notices work items that have sat untouched for about two weeks (10 business days), decisions that are running 3–5 business days late, and gates that were marked complete without evidence. *Why it matters:* stalled work and overdue calls surface on their own, instead of waiting to be discovered.

Net effect: project records manage their own lifecycle under clear rules, the platform keeps one honest catalog of its skills and artifacts, and quiet or overdue work gets surfaced automatically.

## For operators / builders

- The entity-lifecycle transition protocols are in `core/standards/entity-lifecycle-protocol.md` and `core/standards/entity-lifecycle-protocol-shared-portfolio.md`; the canonical state vocabulary is `core/standards/lifecycle-states-canonical.md`.
- The G10 write-permission matrix is `core/schemas/entity-field-lifecycle-matrix.md`; the Agent Write Permissions table in `OPERATIONS.md` is aligned to it.
- The artifact-state reconciliation is documented in the new `core/artifact-workflow-protocol.md`; `artifact-generator` and `artifact-lint` were migrated onto the canonical `lifecycle_state` + Domain model and the transitional dual-read path was removed.
- The skill registry is now the platform CMDB at `core/skills/registry.md` (every deployed skill is a configuration item; version and roster-existence are cited from the skill files and `deploy.sh`, never duplicated). This is recorded in **ADR-038**, which supersedes part 4 of **ADR-035** and preserves its other decisions; the skill-router classifies against a role-Specialist-filtered view. The per-project Artifact Register template is `operations/templates/artifact-register-template.md`.
- The project-tracking integrity sweep (dormancy 10bd / overdue-decision 3–5bd / evidence-backed gate completion) lives in `delivery-engine`.

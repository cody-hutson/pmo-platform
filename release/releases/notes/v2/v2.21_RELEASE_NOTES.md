# Decision briefings now show the full picture before asking you to choose

## What changed for everyone

When the release hub reaches a decision point — a Stage 9 go/no-go, a version choice, a scope-lock — it now holds itself to three standards before it asks you anything:

- **It loads the homework first.** Every spec, rule, or register entry the decision rests on is read before the briefing is written — no deciding against a half-remembered summary. *Why it matters:* the decision you render is grounded in what the specs actually say, not the hub's recollection of them.
- **It shows you every option, not just its favorite.** The full option space is laid out — including the option your own past preference implies — so nothing surfaces *after* you have already answered. The complete briefing is printed in chat before the structured prompt appears. *Why it matters:* you never discover a better option only after you have already committed to a choice.
- **It scores each option against the platform's design principles.** A new design-principle register (Scalability, Maintainability, Simplicity, Stability, and the rest) lets the hub mark each option ALIGNED with, or in CONFLICT with, a named principle — the same way it already checks options against upstream conventions. A conflict with a high-stakes principle (e.g., Stability) is flagged for your sign-off rather than quietly accepted. *Why it matters:* trade-offs against the platform's own values become visible and auditable instead of implicit.

You can also set **standing instructions per decision type** — for example, "always offer a 'defer to next release' option at the go/no-go, and always show me the rollback path." These directives only ever *add* to what the hub surfaces; they can never hide an option or a dimension the hub would otherwise have shown you.

Net effect: fewer decisions made on thin information, and a clearer, auditable record of why each option was or was not a good fit.

## For operators / builders

- The 5-gate "Information Sufficiency" contract lives in `hub-spoke-bridge.md` § Operating Principle: Decision Briefing.
- The design-principle register is `core/standards/design-principle-register.md` — index-only (it points at where each principle is already defined, never restating it). A new `deploy.sh --check` Check 45 keeps it honest: every entry resolves to a real source line, and no design-principle reference dangles.
- Per-gate-class framing directives are authored in the milestone description; the schema is in `engagement-charter.md`. They are ADD-only by contract.

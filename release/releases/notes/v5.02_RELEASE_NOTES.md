# Framings state their question's level, and out-of-order decisions are named

2026-11-06 · v5.02

Epic framings now have one agreed way to state the level their question sits at, and a decision made ahead of an open question it depends on is named a framing slip. This release records the rule; nothing checks it automatically yet, and there is nothing you need to do.

> **Skip the rest** unless you write or review an epic's framing, cut an epic into slices, or record design decisions under a framing.

## Who this affects

- Anyone who writes or reviews an epic's framing
- Anyone who cuts an epic into slices and carries its framing down
- Reviewers who record design decisions under an epic or slice framing

## What changed for everyone using the platform

- **A framing states the level of its question in one line.** The line names a level of the work hierarchy — for example a single work item, a milestone, or a project — and the one-sentence question the framing answers. The level is that of the smallest piece of work whose outcome the answer governs, so an epic and its slices often state the same level. *Why it matters:* "altitude" already meant several different things on the platform; a framing now says which level it means, in the hierarchy's own terms, instead of adding another meaning.

- **A framing lists its open questions in the order they must be answered.** Each open question names who owns it and which other open questions it waits on, and each decision recorded under the framing names the question it answers. *Why it matters:* the order between questions is written down, so a reviewer can see when a decision gets ahead of a question it depends on.

- **A decision taken ahead of an open question it depends on is named a framing slip.** A decision on a question whose earlier question is still open, or one that quietly assumes the answer to another open question, is surfaced to that question's owner. It is not a slip when the decision says so up front: it is marked provisional until the earlier question is settled and re-checked then, it is handed to the slice by name when the epic is cut, or the stage making it moves the question and explains why. A slip is surfaced, never blocked. *Why it matters:* the pattern that went unnoticed before — a design criticised for deciding too early, then repeating the same mistake one level up — now has a name and a test a reviewer can apply.

## Known limits

- Nothing runs the slip test yet. The framing method that would ask for the altitude line, the question order and a test record per decision has not been built; that follow-on work is drafted from this decision record under [#8100](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/8100). Until then the check is a recorded gap in the platform's register of obligations, not a gate.
- The work hierarchy is coarse below a milestone: it stops at a single work item, so two questions inside one work item cannot be told apart by level. The order between questions, not the level, is what the slip test reads.
- No case drawn from the platform's own records is surfaced unconditionally, and no framing yet declares a question order, so the main form of the slip has no real instance to test against. An independent coder agreed with the recorded verdicts on 12 of 15 cases; the disagreements all sit on one boundary, between a conditional verdict and an undetermined one, and a reviewer tiebreak for that boundary is follow-on work.
- Nothing needs installing: no skill, template or hook changed. The platform's recorded version moves to v5.02 when this close lands on the main line.

### Operator and engineering detail

**The decision record (#7965).** ADR-230 decides three things that ADR-211 left open: the form of a framing's altitude declaration, the measure behind "decided one level below its question", and where a slip is detected. The altitude term is ADR-211's, unchanged: a value of the work-item type schema's `general_level` enum, assigned by the smallest entity whose outcome the question's answer governs. "One level below" reads as one step in the framing's declared question order, which is framing content, not a new dependency edge between work items. The cut-basis card's altitude entry uses the same term. The record reads `Proposed` until the operator ratifies it at this release's close; once `Accepted` it is changed only by a superseding record.

The declaration and one decision record, in the form the record sets:

```text
**Altitude:** Milestone/Workstream — <the question this framing answers, one sentence>
Q1 — <question> · owner <who> · waits on none · open
Q2 — <question> · owner <who> · waits on Q1 · provisional on Q1
[FRAMING-SLIP: d3 on Q2; predecessors Q1 open; presupposes none → declared (a)]
```

**Where detection lives.** The test is a review-time judgment split by moment: at framing, at the cut, and when a stage records a design decision under a framing. No runner exists yet, so the check is registered in `core/standards/gate-efficacy-standard.md` as a class 3-O named gap, with its declared observable (the per-decision test records) and a population, `framing-slip-test-records`, that each release close measures. The design-review checklist item and the Stage-4 re-review line that would host the design-time arm are named as seams for R6's build card.

**The cases.** The record applies its definition to 15 cases: the measured instance from the epic's own evidence (surfaced if its framing had enumerated the channel question; the critic's repeat is undetermined from the quoted text), ADR-184's too-low cut (conditional), ADR-173's kind-token alternative (not surfaced: the question it presupposed was already answered), out-of-sample and must-not-surface cases, and a declared ladder move under ADR-211's continuity rule (not surfaced). A blind re-code by the Stage 8 reviewer agreed on 12 of 15 against a threshold of 12.

**How it shipped.** One card on one branch: Engineering Commit 0, the record, and the register row, then a Stage 7 fix and a Stage 9 condition fix that recorded the blind re-code. At Stage 12 the record's two deferred number citations were stamped to `ADR-230` before the merge; the release merged at `1c985c2f` and claimed `v5.02` with a signed annotated tag, equal to its provisional number.

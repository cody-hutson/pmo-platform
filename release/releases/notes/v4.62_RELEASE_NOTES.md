# A pack that says nothing about a kind it declares is now findable

2026-09-11 · v4.62

A methodology pack could declare a work-item kind and then fill in nothing for it — no fields, no criteria — and every check the platform ran called it fine. This release writes down what that state means, ships a check that finds it, and fills in the one gate the Kanban pack had declared and left blank.

> **Skip the rest** unless you author or review a methodology pack, or read what the platform's deploy checks report.

## Who this affects

- Anyone authoring or reviewing a methodology pack or kit
- Anyone reading the platform's deploy-check output

## What changed for everyone using the platform

- **A kind that declares nothing now has a recorded answer, and it is two answers rather than one.** A work-item kind with no fields and no criteria passes the pack structure check permanently — that verdict is not provisional and will never be graded into a failure — while a separate check reports the same kind as incomplete content. *Why it matters:* you can tell which of the two checks a complaint came from, and scaffolding a pack ahead of its content stops looking identical to finishing it.

- **Writing down that nothing belongs on a surface is a complete answer.** What the platform now looks for is silence — a surface the author left out altogether — and not emptiness: a list written as empty, with a stated reason for being empty, is a position taken and stays green. *Why it matters:* you are never pushed to invent content to satisfy a check, and the one-line way to answer a surface you genuinely owe nothing on is a construct the pack grammar already gave you.

- **A new check reports packs that scaffold a kind and leave it unauthored.** It ships logged but not blocking, and it carries its own pair of test packs — one silent, one complete — that have to keep telling each other apart or the check itself is reported broken. *Why it matters:* a shortfall is visible from the day a pack is scaffolded rather than whenever someone next reads the pack, and nobody's deploy turns red while the packs are still being written.

- **The Kanban pack's work-in-progress gate is written out instead of left blank.** The pack now states the rule in full — a card cannot be pulled into in-progress while its container already holds as many in-progress cards as its limit allows — and expresses class of service by which kinds the count covers rather than by a set of labels nobody agreed to. *Why it matters:* the practice the pack claims to describe is actually in the pack now, so a deployment reading it gets the rule rather than an empty placeholder where the rule should be.

- **The pack corpus declares its first setting a deployment is expected to supply.** The work-in-progress cap is declared as a named control with its type and its allowed range, carrying the practice it comes from and deliberately carrying no default. *Why it matters:* the cap is still yours to choose — the pack says a limit exists and what shape it takes, and your deployment says what the number is.

## Known limits

- **Nothing in the platform evaluates that gate today, and this release does not change that.** The pack declares what the method prescribes and what a deployment must supply; reading that declaration and enforcing it when someone moves a card is a deployment's own adapter, and no such adapter exists here. Writing the rule down is the whole of what shipped — nothing starts stopping anyone from pulling a card.
- The cap has no value anywhere in the platform, by design. A deployment that never supplies one leaves the gate with nothing to compare against.
- The new content check reports; it does not fail a deploy. It becomes a hard failure by one committed setting, and the intent is to make that change once enough packs exist for the finding to be worth stopping on — which is the same work that will produce most of the findings.
- The check reads whether an author took a position on a surface. It cannot judge whether the position is correct against the practice it names; that stays a reading a person does.
- Eight follow-ups were opened during this release rather than absorbed into it — among them a parsing fault in the pack reader, two declared fields nothing enforces, and a recovery command the release tooling prints that cannot run as printed.

Report issues at https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues.

### Operator and engineering detail

**The posture is two rule sets that never meet, and the boundary between them is a predicate rather than a convention.** A rule belongs to grammar-conformance when its verdict is computable from a pack's structure alone — the presence, type, shape and closed-domain membership of a key. It belongs to content-completeness when its verdict requires reading what a declaration says, or observing that a declaration is absent where the author owed a position. The discriminator is whether the rule needs to open a declaration's interior: the `PACK-*` set never does, and every `PACKC-*` predicate does. That keeps a content finding from disturbing the grammar check's own discrimination control, which is the failure the silence-versus-emptiness distinction exists to avoid — a rule keyed on emptiness would fire on the very artifact the grammar created to make emptiness auditable, and would turn the pack-grammar check red on both of its control arms.

**The content check is a sibling over a shared pack reader, not an extension of the grammar check**, and the siting decision is recorded as its own architecture decision record rather than left implicit. It registers as `deploy.sh` Check 80 with three rule families: kind-scoped (*did the author take a position on this surface*), entry-scoped (*is each position attributed*), and entry-shape (*does each entry carry the declared tuple with in-domain values*). Its own discrimination fixtures are asserted before its findings are believed — a silent pack must exit 1 naming `PACKC-C01` and a complete pack must exit 0, or the check reports itself as no longer discriminating rather than reporting the corpus as clean. Graduation to enforcement is one committed `resolve_check_mode` token; the flip criterion is recorded as a row in the gate-coverage register, and the archetype-breadth work that supplies the firing population and that graduation are the same event.

**The controls declaration is the corpus's first, and it is what a deployment reads to learn what it must configure.** Exactly one `[[controls]]` block exists across the three shipped packs; the second occurrence anywhere in the tree is the complete-kind test fixture this release added. The gate resolves its threshold through the control by reference rather than by an inlined number:

```toml
[[controls]]
control_id   = "kanban-wip-limit-in-progress"
display_name = "WIP Limit — In Progress"
[controls.value_domain]
type = "integer"
min  = 0
[controls.applies_to]
levels = ["Milestone/Workstream", "Work Item"]
kinds  = ["card"]
```

**The gate is declarative and there is no evaluator, which is stated here because a reader would otherwise assume one.** Across 264 tracked code files, the identifiers the gate condition is built from — its limit reference, its unresolved-disposition, its guarded transition, and the disposition value itself — occur **zero** times outside the pack manifest and the check's own test fixtures. Four design values were settled by reading the Kanban Method rather than by platform convention: no default on the control, a floor of zero rather than one, a health warning rather than a hard transition block when the cap cannot be resolved, and a container scope rather than a board scope. That provenance is recorded on the gate entry, on the controls declaration, and in the control-declaration decision record — not in the plan's practice-sourcing label, which reports only where the deliverable's files live. The release plan's deviation log records that ruling as a visibly declined directive rather than a silently retained one, because three reviewers independently found its absence.

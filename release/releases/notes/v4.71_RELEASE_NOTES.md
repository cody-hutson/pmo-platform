# Each kind of work now names the design questions a framing of it must answer

2026-10-02 · v4.71

An epic framing can now check which design questions it owes before the epic is cut into cards, instead of finding a gap after someone names it. A new decision record names eight work natures — how a piece of work relates to what already exists — and, for each nature, the design axes a framing of that work must answer.

> **Skip the rest** unless you frame epics, slice them into cards, or plan and review work cut from a framed epic.

## Who this affects

- Anyone who frames an epic or slices one into cards
- Anyone who triages, plans or reviews work cut from an epic framed against the model
- The workspace owner, who records each framing measured against the model at the close of the milestone that delivered it

## What changed for everyone using the platform

- **Eight work natures, each with a definition and a boundary example.** A defect, a new capability, a data-structure change, an integration change, an investigation, an infrastructure change, a restructure and a behaviour change are each defined, with an example on each side of the line, and a written procedure picks one by classifying each acceptance criterion. *Why it matters:* the cut patterns and the design-axis toolkit still to come share this one list, so they cannot grow two different vocabularies for the same work.

- **Each nature names the design axes it owes.** Four axes — data, process flow, structure and placement, and behaviour over time — each taken from established architecture practice, and a table of which ones each nature owes. *Why it matters:* a framing can see which design questions its work must answer before it is cut; the epic that prompted this release answered only its structure on the first pass, and its flow and data questions surfaced only when an operator named them.

- **A framing states one of three outcomes for each owed axis, and carries into the slices cut from it.** Each owed axis reads answered, declared not applicable with a reason, or silent, and the slices of an epic account for every axis the epic owed. *Why it matters:* a skipped design question shows up as silent instead of disappearing, and a later stage cannot change the framing without saying so.

- **The model states how much evidence stands behind each row.** One row is proven against a 31-item sample of past work — defects — and the other seven are hypotheses, with a stated trigger for re-deciding the model as new framings are recorded. *Why it matters:* you can tell which rows rest on delivered work and which are a starting assumption that the next framings will test.

## Known limits

- Nothing reads the model yet. The cut-pattern catalog, the design-axis toolkit and the epic-framing surface that will use it are separate cards still to be built; until they ship, applying the model is a matter of practice, and no check enforces it.
- Seven of the eight rows are hypotheses, and the one proven row rests on governance-class work only. Whether the kind of deliverable changes which axes a nature owes could not be tested on the sample, so one key is kept as the stated default.
- The model's two comparisons — each new framing against the table, and each slice's framing against its parent's — have no tool behind them. Each is a dated line the workspace owner adds at a milestone close, and the slice comparison cannot start until slices carry their framing.
- The worked example in the record, the epic that prompted the release, is a consistency check on the case the model came from, not a test of it. The test is the next framings.
- The record is now ratified and is not edited in place: changing the natures, the axes or the table takes a new record that replaces it.

Report issues at https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues.

## Reversibility

MODERATE / HIGH. Reverting the single release merge removes the record and its register rows, and nothing yet depends on them. Once the cards that build on the model ship, a change takes a replacing record instead, at a cost that grows with them. The version tag stays in place as the record that the version was claimed.

### Operator and engineering detail

**The record.** [ADR-211](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/blob/main/core/ADRs/ADR-211-design-axes-and-cut-patterns-key-on-work-nature.md) keys the platform's design axes and its cut patterns on one classification. It defines eight natures: the six it set out with, plus `restructure` and `behaviour-change`, each admitted from a candidate under the admission rule the record fixed before coding. The classification procedure runs per acceptance criterion, first yes wins, and the item takes the nature most of its criteria carry; a criterion that verifies no change is not classified. A crosswalk reconciles the key with the classifications the platform already had — the intake type map, the requirement types, the deliverable-class sense of `domain`, methodology kinds and `type:` labels — rather than extending one of them. The four axes are sourced from Zachman, Kruchten's 4+1 views, arc42, UML 2.5, BABOK v3 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010.

**The shape the record fixes.** One row of the owed-axis table, and the dated framing line the calibration gap is observed through, as the record and the gate-efficacy register state them:

```text
| Nature | data | process-flow | structure-placement | behaviour-over-time | Row |
| `defect` | — | owed | owed | — | proven — 6 delivered items, Governance and Data strata; deliverable class `governance` only (2 of the 6 inferred) |
framing N · YYYY-MM-DD · <nature> · <concept-1 class> · <axes needed> · <axes declared not applicable, with reasons>
```

**The evidence.** A 31-item validation set was drawn by a seeded, stratified draw from 256 improvement-form filings (Governance 226, Software 23, Data 7) and graded from E0 to E3 on delivered evidence, where E3-F marks a framing an operator had to correct. No item carried E3-F, so the owed-axis rule moved no mark. Stage 8 re-coded the first nine items blind from the codebook alone and agreed on 8 of 9 natures (6 of the 7 left once the two items the codebook was refined on are set aside), above the floor of seven that would have reset every row to hypothesis.

**The registered gaps.** Two class-3-O rows in `core/standards/gate-efficacy-standard.md`'s gate-coverage register record the calibration comparison (three limbs: under-owed, unnamed axis, over-owed) and the continuity rule. Neither has a runner, so neither carries a resolution pointer; each names the workspace owner and a dated-line observable, and a Version History row records the addition. No code changed, and Check 62's verdict and count are unchanged.

**How it shipped.** One spike card produced the record, the two register rows and the Version History row on one branch, landed as one two-parent merge over four files. After the Stage 9 GO a sibling release merged first, claiming this release's provisional version and ADR numbers 207 to 210; that voided the GO on its version arm, and the operator chose to re-baseline, re-verify and merge. The renumber tool moved the record from ADR-207 to ADR-211, and the claim at the merge returned `v4.71` on the first attempt, as a signed annotated tag. The record was ratified Accepted at the Stage 13 gate by a decision rendered for it alone. Nothing was deployed: decision records and standards have no installed copy.

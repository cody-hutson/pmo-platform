# Grouping backlog items into epics now has a decided home and one way in

2026-11-08 · v5.04

A decision record, Accepted at this close, says where the method for grouping many backlog items into epics lives and how it will be started. Nothing is built in this release and there is nothing you need to do: the method, its starting point and its filing step come in a later release that can now be planned against this decision.

> **Skip the rest** unless you group backlog items into epics or initiatives, plan the work that builds the composition method, or review designs that touch the release planner, the release executor or the intake desk.

## Who this affects

- Anyone who groups loose backlog items into epics, or works out which epics an initiative needs
- Whoever plans the follow-on work that builds the method, the design-axis toolkit or the decomposition method, all three of which this decision answers
- Reviewers of designs that touch the release planner, the release executor or the intake desk

## What changed for everyone using the platform

- **Where epic composition lives is decided.** The method for grouping many backlog items into epics will be one shared method document in the platform core, cited by the skills that run it and copied by none; the [decision record](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/blob/main/core/ADRs/ADR-233-composition-method-is-core-and-enters-through-the-release-planner.md) explains why the core is the only home every part of the platform can cite. *Why it matters:* the work that builds the method can now be planned against a named home instead of an open question.

- **There will be one way in, reached by plain requests.** The method will be started through one mode on the release planner, which picks what to run from what you are grouping — a milestone, a set of work items, or an initiative — and five phrasings such as "compose an epic from these items" are recorded for it. *Why it matters:* once it is built, you can start a composition without knowing which skill hosts it.

- **Nothing is filed until you approve the exact drafts.** The mode only proposes: it writes one proposal holding the groupings, the reasoning and the literal draft of every new card, each draft checked for readiness first, and a separate step that you start files exactly those drafts and reads them back. *Why it matters:* what reaches the tracker is word for word what you approved.

- **Groupings are kept in every methodology.** Where your methodology has no grouping kind of work item — Kanban today, and Scrum until its epic is declared as one — each member is tagged with a grouping label instead of the grouping being lost. *Why it matters:* composition does not depend on one methodology's vocabulary.

- **Two related questions get the same answer on where a method lives.** The decomposition method, which splits large items, will also be a core method document, and the design-axis toolkit will be a separate core reference that both methods point to. *Why it matters:* the follow-on work for both can cite this one decision instead of deciding the question again.

## Known limits

- Nothing is built in this release. The method document, the release-planner mode and the filing step are the work of the [codification story](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues/6389), which this decision unblocks for planning; until it ships, the five phrasings reach nothing new.
- The Scrum methodology does not yet declare its epic as a grouping kind. Until that declaration lands, Scrum groupings will be carried on a label rather than a native parent, and in Kanban they stay on a label for as long as it declares only cards.
- Above the epic, nothing is filed as an item: an initiative stays a grouping label plus a roadmap, as an earlier Accepted decision requires.
- The check that the five phrasings do not compete with other skills compares words, so a request phrased differently with the same meaning can still reach another skill.

Report issues at https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/issues, and report a suspected vulnerability privately as the [Security Policy](https://github.com/cody-hutson/pmo-platform/blob/main/SECURITY.md) describes.

## Reversibility

MODERATE / MEDIUM. Before this close the record could be revised in place, or the release merge reverted. Now that it is Accepted, the decision changes only through a record that supersedes it, at a cost that grows as the follow-on work builds on it. Confidence is high on the home and medium on the entry point, which was a judgment between two candidates that both survived review. The version tag is kept as the record that the version was claimed.

### Operator and engineering detail

**The decision record (#6390).** ADR-233 places the composition method in `core/disciplines/` on the root-cause-analysis precedent. Its extend-before-create determination reads `net-new because in-place is infeasible` (ADR-094; build-philosophy row 3): the one covering surface, the bundle-composition doctrine, is a release-module standard scoped to milestone bundles, and the module boundary (ADR-007) admits a surface citable from both consumer modules only in core. The entry is one thin, read-only, altitude-dispatching mode on `release-planner` that persists one composition proposal as a handoff file; every write sits in one operator-explicit execute step on `release-executor`, which validates the proposal, snapshots, files each approved body verbatim, wires membership, reads both back and routes the rest to a disposition queue. The altitude parameter reads ADR-211's ladder, refined at the Work Item level by the kind's declared `level_role` (ADR-180), and never branches on a kind name. ADR-049 governs the tier above the epic, so a kind there is conditional on the deferred grouping-tier card reconciling with ADR-049 Decision 1, or on a superseding record. The record also carries the per-capability boundary table (ADR-019), the three entry points it does not change, and the codification story's edit set — with the Scrum `level_role = "grouping"` declaration, and every reader it moves, as a precondition.

**The trigger phrasing.** The five phrases were scored with the ADR-114 trigger-collision gate's own primitives over all 56 skills: as a skill of their own they peak at 0.048, and added to the release planner its highest pair is 0.094, against an escalate threshold of 0.30. QA re-ran the measurement independently and reproduced every figure.

**How it shipped.** One card on one branch. The Stage 5 design went through an adversarial review whose two Major findings — the record omitted ADR-049's representation of the tier above the epic, and its write path named the planner-to-executor precedent without adopting that precedent's persisted handoff and verbatim filing — were resolved by a bounded revision under the operator's scope lock. Dev Testing ran reduced to document-conformance checks by operator decision; QA graded all six acceptance criteria MET; Plan Review returned GO with one condition, every required check green before the merge, which was met at Execute. Three releases opened after this one's first commit merged ahead of it and took ADR-230 to ADR-232 and versions v5.02 and v5.03, so the record was renumbered from 230 to 233 at merge and the release claimed v5.04. It merged at `60e2369c` with a signed annotated tag; the only deploy was the rules-mirror refresh, because no skill, package, hook or rules-mirror member changed. The record was ratified `Proposed → Accepted` at this close under the operator's standing close-out direction.
